# Japanese Dashi & Seasoning Pantry

Companion to `japanese-cuisine-technique/SKILL.md`. What each Japanese staple
does, when to add it, and how to substitute. Sources cited at bottom.

## Dashi types (pick by dish)

- **Awase (kombu + katsuobushi)** — the everyday standard. Deep umami from the
  glutamate/IMP synergy. Use for most soups, sauces, simmered dishes.
- **Kombu dashi** (kombu only) — delicate, vegan. Clear soups, light dishes.
- **Katsuo dashi** (bonito only) — robust, savory. Heartier soups.
- **Iriko dashi** (dried sardines/anchovies) — Kyushu-style, hearty, slightly
  bitter-fishy. The classic base for rich miso soups.
- **Shiitake dashi** — vegan, earthy. Vegetarian dishes.
- **Vegan dashi** — shiitake + kombu. Plant-based awase equivalent.
- Cold-brew the kombu (see technique skill) for the cleanest, brightest stock.

## Shoyu (soy sauce) grades

- **Koikuchi** — dark, standard, ~80% of Japanese soy. All-purpose.
- **Usukuchi** — LIGHTER in color but SALTIER (Kansai). Use in clear soups and
  nimono where you want salt without darkening the broth.
- **Tamari** — near-gluten-free, thick, rich. Dipping / sauces.
- **Saishikomi** — double-brewed, intense, premium. Finishing / sashimi.
- Use Japanese soy specifically — its flavor profile differs from Chinese and
  Korean soy.

## Mirin vs sake

- **Sake** — alcohol. Tenderizes meat/fish, deodorizes, adds umami. Added
  EARLY (with or just before sugar) so the alcohol cooks off.
- **Mirin** — sweet rice wine. Adds shine, sweetness, and tempers salt. Two
  kinds matter:
  - **Hon-mirin** (~14% alcohol, real) → add EARLY, with sake.
  - **Mirin-fu** (mirin-style sweetened condiment) → add LAST for gloss.
- **Mirin substitute:** 1 Tbsp sake + 1 tsp sugar.

## Miso types

- **Shiro / white** — short ferment, sweet, low salt (Kyoto). Dressings, light
  soups, fish marinades.
- **Aka / red** — long ferment, bold, salty (Nagoya/Sendai). Stews, dengaku,
  hearty miso soup.
- **Awase** (white + red blend) — the everyday default. Balanced.
- **Shinshu** (Nagano) — balanced, most exported. Great general-purpose.
- **Mugi** (barley koji, Kyushu) — nutty.
- **Hatcho** (100% soybean, aged years) — near-black, intense, almost bitter.
  Use sparingly.
- **Rule:** never boil miso — dissolve it off the heat (~1 Tbsp per
  150–200 ml dashi) so its aroma survives.

## Seasoning order (sa-shi-su-se-so)

Sake (early) → sugar → salt → vinegar → soy (near end) → miso (last, off-heat).
Hon-mirin rides in early with sake; mirin-fu and glossy tare go on at the end.
