# Korean Cuisine — Technique

Load when cooking or teaching Korean dishes: bibimbap, galbi/bulgogi, kimchi jjigae, doenjang jjigae, japchae, ssam (wraps), tteokbokki, banchan, or stir-fried noodles. Companion pantry reference: `skills/culinary-techniques/korean-spice-pantry/SKILL.md`.

## Core principles

1. **Five-flavour map.** Every Korean dish lives on balance of: gochujang (hot-sweet), doenjang (savoury umami), ganjang/Korean soy sauce (salty), vinegar (sour), and toasted sesame oil + garlic + green onion (the aromatic layer). A dish should never feel edgy on one axis — always balanced.

2. **Fermentation is the engine.** The jangs (doenjang, ganjang, gochujang) share a *meju* (soybean-brick) fermentation origin. Kimchi is lacto-fermented — salt % and temperature control the LAB (lactic acid bacteria) activity. The cured, sour, funky notes are the flavour foundation.

3. **Bap + banchan + jang.** No Korean meal is one dish — it's rice (bap) with a spread of small sides (banchan), kimchi always present, and a jang-based condiment. The table is participatory: wraps assembled at the table, stews bubbling in ttukbaegi, rice mixed vigorously in a stone bowl.

4. **Sesame oil is always a finish.** Toasted sesame oil (*chamgireum*) is a flavour-finisher, never a cooking oil — it scorches and turns bitter on high heat. Drizzle at the very end.

5. **Each component seasoned separately.** In banchan and bibimbap, every vegetable gets its own brief seasoning — that's the colour/texture contrast point. Combining them makes the dish; cooking them together makes porridge.

## Numbered techniques (specifics)

### 1. Ssam — whole-leaf wraps & etiquette
Red-leaf or green-leaf lettuce (*sangchu*) + perilla leaves (*kkaennip*), washed, drained, leaves whole. Each eater builds their own wrap at the table: one leaf base → grilled meat → smear of ssamjang → optional rice → optional raw garlic slice or perilla → fold and one-bite. Lettuce = crunchy palate-cleanser; perilla = mint-piney aroma. Common ssam vegetables: lettuce, perilla, blanched cabbage, steamed zucchini leaves, fresh seaweed.

**Ssamjang = the bond.** Per Korean Bapsang: 2 tbsp doenjang : 1 tbsp gochujang (2:1, umami-forward), 1 tsp minced garlic, ½ tbsp sugar (or honey), 2 tsp toasted sesame oil, 1 tsp sesame seeds, 1 tbsp water/rice wine to thin. Stir smooth. Optional: finely chopped onion, scallion, green chilli, toasted nuts. Ratio favours more doenjang than gochujang by default. For a cooked version, sauté onion+garlic+scallion in oil, stir in doenjang+gochujang+protein, thin with water.

**Pitfall:** ssamjang without enough doenjang is just sweet-chilli paste — the savoury foundation is what makes a wrap a wrap.

### 2. Muchim / quick pickles — blanching times & dressings
Every muchim follows: blanch → shock → squeeze dry → dress. Soggy banchan is the #1 failure — squeeze means squeeze.

- **Spinach muchim:** blanch in boiling salted water **30 sec–1 min**, shock in cold water, squeeze dry. Dress with 1 tsp soy + 1 tsp sesame oil + pinch salt + minced garlic + sesame seeds.
- **Kongnamul (bean sprouts muchim):** boil **5–7 min** (Maangchi: uncovered then covered), shock, squeeze. Season with soy + sesame oil + salt + scallion.
- **Zucchini / firm veg:** blanch or stir-fry **~2 min** until translucent. Same dress-and-squeeze method.
- **Reuse the pot** (Maangchi method): blanch in order of colour-least-to-most — spinach first, then noodles, then veg.

### 3. Marinade science — bulgogi/galbi ratios & enzymes
Working ratio per **~450 g (1 lb) thin-sliced beef** (Korean Bapsang bulgogi):
- 4–6 tbsp soy sauce, 3 tbsp water, 3–4 tbsp sugar (or honey)
- 2 tbsp rice wine/mirin, 2 tbsp minced garlic (5–6 cloves)
- 2 tbsp toasted sesame oil, 2 tsp sesame seeds
- ½ grated Korean/Asian pear (or apple), ⅛ tsp pepper

**Marinating times (non-negotiable)**
- Thin-sliced (bulgogi): **30 min–1 hr** (up to overnight)
- Thick galbi ribs: **6–8 hrs, overnight to 24 hrs best**

**Enzymatic tenderisers.** Asian pear is the gentle traditional choice. Kiwi and pineapple are MUCH stronger — **½ kiwi max per 2 lbs meat**; for overnight thin cuts, skip fruit entirely or the meat turns to mush. Grated onion and a splash of alcohol also tenderise.

**Doneness cue:** caramelised, slightly charred edges with juicy interior — turn once over medium-high heat, 2–3 min per side. Sugar+fruit marinade scorches easily — cook fast.

### 4. Jang craft — the three born together
Soybeans soak overnight, boil, pound into *meju* bricks, ferment on rice straw. In spring, bricks aged in brine (onggi crocks). The strained liquid → boiled → **ganjang** (soy sauce). The mashed solids → **doenjang**. **Gochujang** is a separate ferment: gochugaru + glutinous rice + meju/soybean powder + salt, aged months to years.

**Cooking with jang:** don't simmer at a hard rolling boil for the whole duration. Add early to stews to dissolve and bloom, but keep the pot below violent boil so flavours integrate without scorching. Doenjang/ssamjang: bloom in hot fat/with meat EARLY before adding liquid.

### 5. Banchan building

**Japchae (sweet potato noodles, dangmyeon):**
- Boil **8 min total** (1 min covered, stir, then 7 min covered) for soft+chewy. Drain, cut with scissors.
- Season noodles: 1 tsp soy + 2 tsp sesame oil + 1 tsp sugar
- Stir-fry EACH vegetable SEPARATELY (onion 2 min, mushrooms 2 min, carrot 20 sec, beef a few min)
- Combine: 1 tbsp soy + 1 tbsp sugar + ½ tsp pepper + 2 tsp sesame oil + sesame seeds
- **Key:** cooking each component separately gives the signature colour/texture. Overcook = mushy grey noodles.

**Gyeran mari (rolled egg omelette):**
- 4 eggs + 1 tsp salt + minced onion/veg
- Low heat (medium-low to low). Pour in THIRDS.
- Each layer **1–2 min** until set on bottom but still runny on top
- Roll, repeat. Finish rolling all sides **3–4 min** on low.
- Cool 5 min, slice **½–¾ inch**.
- **Pitfall:** high heat = brown, tough egg roll — low and slow is the entire method.

**Kkakdugi (cubed radish kimchi):**
- Cut radish into ¾–1-inch cubes
- Salt with **3 tbsp coarse sea salt per 5.5 lbs** (≈2.5 tbsp kosher) for **~1 hr**, toss every 20 min
- **Drain WITHOUT rinsing** — discard the salty brine only
- Rub **½ cup gochugaru** into cubes for colour, then add 2 tbsp fish sauce + 3 tbsp saeujeot (salted shrimp) + garlic + ginger + sugar + optional pear/rice
- Ferment room temp **2–3 days** (longer than cabbage kimchi), then fridge; best after **1–2 weeks**

### 6. Korean BBQ — galbi & ssamgyeopsal

**LA galbi** (flanken cut, ~⅓-inch sliced across bones):
- Marinade per #3, **6–24 hrs**
- Grill: **medium-high heat, 2–3 min per side**, turn once — charred-caramelised
- Broil: **5–6 min + 3–4 min flipped**, 6 inches below element
- Pan-fry: quick-braise WITH the marinade (extra liquid stops the sugar scorching)
- **Pitfall:** sugar-heavy marinade scorches before the inside is cooked on dry high heat. Medium-high + liquid nearby is the guard.

**Ssamgyeopsal (unmarinated pork belly, ⅓–½ inch thick):**
- **UNMARINATED** — straight thick-sliced pork belly
- Sear over **high heat** until golden-crisp on both sides with rendered fat
- Roast kimchi and garlic in the rendered fat
- Wrap with lettuce/perilla + ssamjang + pickled radish

### 7. Kimchi at home
**Salting:** **2–3% salt by weight** (Maangchi: ½ cup kosher salt per 6 lbs cabbage; coarse Korean sea salt draws moisture without over-salting). Salt leaves, more toward thick stems, rest **2 hrs**, flip every 30 min, until white stems bend without snapping.

**Rinse:** 3–4 times in cold water, drain cut-side down **30–45 min**.

**Paste:** gochugaru + garlic + ginger + fish sauce (*myeolchi-aekjeot*) + saeujeot (fermented shrimp) + optional sweet-rice-glue (muk/rice porridge) + optional pear/plum syrup.

**Ferment:** **1–2 days room temp** (65–70°F / 18–21°C; warmer=faster, saltier=slower) until bubbling and tangy, then fridge. Salt + anaerobiosis selects LAB → pH drops below ~4.6 = preserved + sour.

### 8. Soup/stew technique — when the jang goes in

**Kimchi jjigae (kimchi stew):**
- Use **OLD sour kimchi** — past its prime is the right time.
- **Sauté FIRST:** kimchi + fatty pork + gochugaru + garlic in oil **5–7 min** to develop flavour.
- Add kimchi juice + **2–2.5 cups water/rice-rinse water** (*ssalddeumul*, 2nd–3rd rinse — thickens broth).
- Boil 5 min, simmer covered 15 min.
- Add tofu + scallion, boil **~5 min** until tofu is cooked through.
- **Pitfall:** fresh kimchi in jjigae = flat, sweet, no sour depth. Use old kimchi.

**Doenjang jjigae (soybean paste stew):**
- **Bloom the paste:** sauté meat + **2 tbsp doenjang + 1 tsp gochugaru** in oil over medium **3–4 min** — this activates the paste's aroma.
- Add 2 cups water, boil 3–4 min.
- Add veg + tofu, boil 5–6 min.
- Finish with scallion + optional **1 tsp vinegar** a minute or two before serving (brightens the doenjang).

**Sundubu jjigae (soft tofu stew):**
- Same bloom-the-paste logic (gochugaru/gochujang base).
- Add soft tofu GENTLY + egg.
- Simmer, don't stir vigorously — the unctuous soft tofu stays in pieces.
- **Doneness cue:** bubbling actively, served still bubbling from a *ttukbaegi* (earthenware pot).

### 9. Rice & noodles

**Bibimbap bowl building:**
- Hot short-grain rice base
- Individual seasoned veg (*namul*) arranged in SEPARATE sections (one wedge each — no mixing on the plate)
- Beef + fried egg (often raw yolk for dolsot, cooks from the stone heat)
- Gochujang dolloped on top
- **Key:** prepare each topping separately with its own brief seasoning — that's the entire point of bibimbap.

**Dolsot (stone pot) for *nurungji* crust (the rice crust):**
- Preheat empty stone pot over medium **8 min** (or medium-high 5–10 min) until sizzling-hot.
- Coat bottom+sides with **~2 tsp toasted sesame oil** (should sizzle immediately).
- Pack in warm rice pressing firmly against walls for full contact.
- Pile toppings on top — DO NOT stir.
- Cook undisturbed on medium another **5–8 min** until you hear cracking = crust forming.
- **Pitfall:** rice that doesn't touch the oiled surface won't crust. Press it in.

**Korean fried rice:**
- Kimchi + old rice over **high heat** until grains are separate and edge-fried.
- Finish with fried egg + sesame oil drizzle.

### 10. Tteokbokki (spicy rice cakes)
**Broth:** 4 cups water + 7 large dried anchovies (heads/guts out) + 6×8-inch dried kelp (*dasima*), boil **15 min uncovered**, fish them out (~2½ cups stock remain).

**Sauce:** dissolve **⅓ cup gochujang + 1 tbsp gochugaru + 1 tbsp sugar** together first.

**Cooking:** add rice cakes + green onion + optional fish cakes/hard-boiled eggs. Simmer **10–15 min**, stirring, until cakes are soft-chewy and sauce thickens and looks shiny.

**Frozen tteok** must be thawed and soaked in cold water first.

**Pitfall:** no anchovy-kelp stock = flat, one-note tteokbokki. The stock is what makes it savoury.

## Signature dishes — how the techniques stack

1. **Dolsot Bibimbap** — rice crust (nurungji, #9) + seasoned namul (#5) + gochujang table condiment + sesame oil finish (#1 principle). One bowl shows grain execution, banchan philosophy, and the jang-as-sauce pattern.

2. **Galbi + Ssam** — enzymatic marinade (#3) → grill (#6) → build wraps (#1) with ssamjang (#1). The full Korean BBQ table in one dish: sweet-savoury caramelised beef, cool lettuce crunch, sharp perilla, umami-rich ssamjang.

3. **Kimchi Jjigae** — old fermented kimchi (#7) sautéed first (#8), then simmered with pork + tofu. Uses rice-rinse water for body. Shows the bloom-first principle and how fermentation ages into cooking.

## Pitfalls (cross-cutting)

- **Scorched gochujang/doenjang:** never fry paste on a dry ripping-hot pan. Bloom in oil at medium, with liquid nearby, then add broth.
- **Clumpy gochugaru:** dissolve flakes into the liquid component or rub into salted veg before adding wet seasonings — never dump dry into simmering liquid.
- **Soggy japchae:** 8 min boil max (not 10+); squeeze blanched veg dry; season each component separately so nothing steams the others.
- **Over-salted kimchi:** weigh the salt (2–3% by cabbage weight, not eyeball); use coarse salt; skip extra salt in the fish sauce/shrimp if you're heavy-handed.
- **Burnt galbi/bulgogi:** sugar+fruit marinade scorches fast. Grill 2–3 min/side, turn once; or pan-fry as a quick-braise with extra marinade as protective liquid.
- **Over-tenderised meat:** kiwi/pineapple is VERY strong — ½ kiwi max per 2 lbs; skip fruit for overnight thin-meat marination.
- **Sesame oil on high heat:** always a finish, never a cooking fat. It scorches and turns bitter.
- **Fresh kimchi in jjigae:** won't work — it hasn't fermented. Old kimchi is the right ingredient.
- **Sundubu broken curds:** don't stir vigorously. Gently slide in soft tofu, simmer, serve.
- **Rice crust fail:** without pressure against oiled stone, no crust. Pack it in.

- **Check `kb/house_rules.md` for the household's dietary restrictions** before
  finalizing any Korean dish. Note: **maesilcheong (green plum syrup) and yuzu
