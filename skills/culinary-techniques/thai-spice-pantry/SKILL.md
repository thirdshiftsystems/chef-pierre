# Thai Spice & Seasoning Pantry

What each regional Thai ingredient does and when in the cook it goes in. Use
with `thai-cuisine-technique` for technique. Sources cited at bottom.

Key: **[HARD]** = hard to substitute. **[SWAP]** = workable substitute. **[TIMING]** =
when in the cook.

## Salty / umami layer

- **Fish sauce (nam pla)** — salt + umami backbone of curries, salads, soups,
  stir-fries, marinades. [HARD — soy 1:1 in a pinch, + Golden Mountain/Maggi for
  sharpness] [TIMING: stage 1 early seasoning, stage 2 at the end after tasting;
  table condiment prik nam pla 3:1 fish-sauce:lime]
- **Oyster sauce (nam mun hoi)** — queen of stir-fry sauces: briny, umami, subtly
  sweet; the pad kra pao sauce base. [SWAP — vegetarian LKK "stir-fry sauce"]
  [TIMING: in the sauce mixture at the end, not the aromatic stage]
- **Soy sauce (see ew kao)** — lighter than Chinese/Japanese; used alongside
  fish sauce for complexity. [SWAP — good Japanese soy 1:1] [TIMING: end-stage
  sauces/marinades]
- **Black soy (see ew dum)** — thick, molasses-dark, mildly salty; color + depth
  for dark stir-fries/braises. [SWAP — Chinese dark soy but saltier: cut back]
  Start small (brands vary hugely).
- **Golden Mountain / seasoning sauce** — richer soy-type, Maggi-like; with soy
  for more complex salt. [SWAP — Maggi/Bragg/soy 1:1]
- **Fermented shrimp paste (gapi/kapi)** — funky umami + salt; in most curry
  pastes in small amounts (you shouldn't be able to ID it); forward in the
  south. [SWAP in pastes — omit + extra fish sauce, or miso for vegan]
  [TIMING: END of paste pounding, just to mix]. Ammonia blast on heat — ventilate.
- **Dried shrimp (kung haeng)** — pungent-salty umami; pounded into som tam,
  fried into pad thai aromatics, some pastes. [SWAP — omit, lean on fish sauce]
  Buy pale-coral, thumbnail-sized, no white powder.
- **Pla ra / padaek** (Isan/north) — chunky fermented fish; the Isan salt+umami
  for som tam (Isan style), nam phrik, soups. [NO swap — it IS the flavor].
  Grill/wrap before using to tame it.
- **Budu** (south) — thick, dark, pungent fish sauce; southern signature.
  [NO swap; closest = nam pla + funk]
- **Fermented soybean paste (tao jiew)** — Thai miso; salty, acidic edge; e.g.
  khao mun gai sauce. [SWAP — miso/doenjang thinned with water]
- **Toasted rice powder (khao khua)** — dry-toast raw sticky rice (optionally
  with makrut leaves) until golden, grind; nutty aroma for laab/Isan salads.
  [HARD in those dishes]

## Sour layer

- **Lime (manao)** — bright acid: salads, dressings, final seasoning.
  [TIMING: ALWAYS after heat — squeeze at table or off-heat]. Never bottled juice.
- **Tamarind (makham piak)** — rounded, cooked-friendly sour: kaeng som,
  massaman, pad thai sauce, som tam (with lime). Thai/Vietnamese product only.

## Sweet layer

- **Palm sugar (nam tan pip)** — caramel/butterscotch sweetener: pad thai, som
  tam, curries. [SWAP — light brown sugar 1:1; white sugar when flavor isn't
  load-bearing] [TIMING: dissolve deliberately — pestle against mortar sides, or
  low heat in the sauce pan]
- **White sugar** — everyday modern sweetener; table condiment #4, stir-fries.

## Heat layer

- **Bird's eye (phrik khi nu)** — fresh, fierce; crushed whole into soups (tom
  yum: 4–5 crushed), pounded into som tam + pad kra pao paste. [SWAP — serrano]
  Deseed to tame.
- **Spur chilies (phrik chee fa)** — mild/medium; fresh for stir-fry color,
  DRIED is the default curry-paste chili (deep red, flavor, mild heat).
  [SWAP — guajillo/California/pasilla dried; anaheim fresh]
- **Hot dried (phrik haeng)** — small, intense; small doses in pastes/nam
  phrik, whole in stir-fries. [SWAP — arbol/japones]

## Aromatics & herbs

- **Galangal (kha)** — piney, cooling, resinous; pounded into pastes, sliced
  paper-thin into soups (tom yum: 7–8 very thin slices). [HARD — ginger is NOT a
  substitute; different aroma]. Freezes perfectly (slice rounds, freeze flat).
- **Lemongrass** — citrus-grassy backbone of pastes, soups, marinades. Use ONLY
  the bottom half; slice thin before pounding; fresh or frozen, NEVER
  dried/powdered in pastes.
- **Makrut lime leaves** — infuse (bruised, torn, big) vs eat (very fine
  julienne, stem removed); ZEST in pastes. [SWAP — lime zest only]. Frozen fresh
  works perfectly.
- **Thai basil (horapa)** — anise-y, purple stems; the curry basil; added OFF
  HEAT to just wilt. [SWAP — Italian basil acceptable in curry; not identical]
- **Holy basil (gaprao/kra pao, tulsi)** — peppery, clove-like, delicate; THE
  basil for pad kra pao; added off-heat, tossed just to wilt. [HARD for the real
  dish; Pailin prefers ITALIAN basil over Thai basil as substitute — closer match]
- **Cilantro/coriander root (rak phak chi)** — the Thai secret; intense cilantro
  flavor, pounded into pastes + marinades. [SWAP — cilantro stems, ~3 stems per
  root]. Leaves added raw at the end, never pounded (flavor can't take cooking).
- **Shallots + garlic** — the moisture layer of pastes (pounded AFTER fibrous
  aromatics — their liquid kills friction); sliced for stir-fry aromatics (fried
  golden ~2 min at medium-high).
- **White peppercorns** — the Thai pepper; floral heat; pounded whole into
  pastes, in grilled-meat marinades. [SWAP — ground white pepper in a pinch]
- **Turmeric** — earthy color/flavor: yellow curry paste, southern curries.
  [SWAP — fresh preferred, ground in a pinch]
- **Grachai (fingerroot)** — the must-have for kaeng som (sour curry). [HARD]
- **Tua nao** — northern fermented soybean cakes; northern pastes instead of
  gapi. [HARD — miso is the workaround]

## Fat / body layer

- **Coconut milk/cream** — first-press UHT, full-fat, no gums; crack it for paste
  frying; layer remaining milk in stages. ~13–17% fat.
- **Nam prik pao (Thai chile jam)** — sweet, umami, mildly spicy paste (dried
  chilies, dried shrimp, shallots, garlic); restaurant-style tom yum, cashew
  chicken, dips. [NO substitute — buy Mae Pranom or make it]
- **Roasted peanuts** — panang paste (richness/thickness), som tam, pad thai
  garnish.
