# Mexican Regional Spice & Seasoning Pantry

Companion to `skills/culinary-techniques/mexican-cuisine-technique/SKILL.md`. Use when shopping for, substituting, or explaining a Mexican ingredient.

## The dried chile system (the flavour backbone)

Dried chiles are the single most important ingredient category in Mexican cooking. They are NOT interchangeable — each has a distinct flavour profile, heat level, and use case. Toast and rehydrate per the technique skill (§4, §5).

### MILD (0–4,000 SHU)

- **Ancho** (dried poblano) — 1,000–2,000 SHU. Prune, raisin, cocoa, mild earth. Wide, thick-walled, reddish-black. Central Mexico. **USE:** base for moles, adobos, salsas. The foundational dried chile.
- **Pasilla** (dried chilaca) — 1,000–2,500 SHU. Raisin, licorice, dried fig, subtle smoke. Thin, long, dark mahogany. **USE:** base layer in moles; adds depth without dominating. **Sub:** 0.8 pasilla per 1 ancho.
- **Mulato** (dried poblano, darker than ancho) — 1,000–2,000 SHU. Earthy, bitter-sweet, cacao, herbs. **USE:** mole poblano.
- **Cascabel** — 1,000–4,000 SHU. Earthy, nutty, mild. Round, reddish-brown, rattle inside. **USE:** sub for chilhuacle when unavailable.
- **Chilhuacle negro** — 1,000–2,000 SHU. Plum, chocolate. Oaxaca. **USE:** signature of mole negro (what gives the black colour when charred).
- **Chilhuacle rojo** — similarly mild, fruity. Used in Oaxacan red moles (coloradito).
- **Chilhuacle amarillo** — similarly mild, slightly tangy. Oaxacan yellow mole.

### MEDIUM (2,500–8,000 SHU)

- **Guajillo** (dried mirasol) — 2,500–5,000 SHU. Fruity, earthy, tea-like, tangy, berry. Large, skinny, bright red, smooth. **USE:** the most-used dried chile overall — salsas, marinades, birria, tamales. The backbone of birria de res (6 guajillo : 4 ancho : 2 pasilla).
- **Chipotle** (smoked dried jalapeño) — 2,500–8,000 SHU. Intensely smoky, sweet, tobacco, dried fruit. Two types: **meco** (light brown, leathery, mild-medium) = sun-dried then smoked; **adobado** = in vinegar-tomato adobo. **USE:** tinga, adobos, salsas, marinades.
- **Costeño** — 5,000–15,000 SHU. Fruity, smoky. Oaxaca/Guerrero coast.

### HOT (10,000–50,000 SHU)

- **Morita** (smoked red jalapeño, smaller than chipotle) — 5,000–10,000 SHU. Smoky, fruity, brighter than chipotle. **USE:** salsas where you want more heat + smoke.
- **Chile de árbol** — 15,000–30,000 SHU. Bright, sharp, grassy heat. Thin, red. **USE:** adds clean heat to salsas and birria; birria's heat source (ario in adobo).
- **Manzano** — 12,000–30,000 SHU. Fruity, apple-like. Black seeds. Usually fresh, not dried.

### EXTREME (100,000+)

- **Habanero** — 100,000–350,000 SHU. Fruity, floral, intensely hot. Yucatán. **USE:** Yucatecan salsas, pickled red onions. The Yucatán heat backbone.
- **Habanero chile seco (dried)** — same profile, more concentrated. Used in Yucatecan recados and long-cooked dishes.

**Chile substitution logic:**
- Ancho ↔ pasilla at 0.8 pasilla : 1 ancho (pasilla is slightly hotter, fruitier)
- Cascabel substitutes for chilhuacle negro when unavailable
- Guajillo is the closest all-purpose sub for other mild-medium dried chiles
- There is NO direct sub for chipotle's smoke — use smoked paprika + guajillo in a pinch (not the same, but closer than anything else)

## Key seasonings & pantry items

### Achiote/annatto (also called bijol)
Earthy, slightly sweet, gives the signature red-orange colour. Ground with spices into **recado rojo** (annatto + oregano, cumin, cloves, allspice, garlic, Yucatecan oregano, bitter orange). **WHEN:** added to marinades early (cochinita pibil, pollo pibil), stirred into rice, tamales. The defining Yucatán seasoning. **Sub:** 1 tbsp achiote paste ± ½ tsp each cumin + oregano.

### Epazote
Pungent, medicinal, slightly citrusy. **The black bean companion** — adds digestibility and reduces gas. Also used in quesadillas, pipián verde, tinga, caldo de frijol. **WHEN:** add NEAR THE END of cooking beans (stir in, 5 min simmer). Too much = bitter.

### Hoja santa (sacred leaf, root beer plant)
Large, heart-shaped leaf, anise/licorice flavour. **USE:** as a tamal wrapper in Oaxaca (tamales oaxaqueños), and to flavour moles, fish, and sauces. **WHEN:** add whole to liquid during cooking, remove before serving. **Sub:** none (banana leaf for wrapper only, won't replicate flavour).

### Mexican oregano (Lippia graveolens)
More earthy, less sweet, with citrus notes than Mediterranean oregano. **USE:** in moles, adobos, birria, recado rojo. **When:** add to dry spice blends early. **Sub:** ½ Mediterranean oregano + ½ marjoram + pinch lemon zest.

### Canela (Mexican cinnamon, Cinnamomum verum soft variety)
Soft, delicate, floral, sweet. **USE:** in moles, champurrado, atole, rice pudding. **NOT:** cassia (harsher, more bitter, not a sub for savoury moles). **Sub:** Ceylon cinnamon (the soft, light-brown quills).

### Mexican vanilla
From the vanilla orchid (Veracruz origin). Richer, more floral than standard extract. **WHEN:** desserts, atole, champurrado. Add at the end.

### Pepitas (hulled raw pumpkin seeds)
The dominant thickener in **pipián** — distinguishes it from mole. **WHEN:** toast in a dry pan until they pop and smell nutty (2–4 min), then grind fine. Add early as a base thickener. Also used as garnish on moles and in *pipián rojo*.

### Sesame (ajonjolí)
Mole thickener and topping. **WHEN:** toast in a dry pan 2 min until fragrant (they pop and smell nutty). Ground into mole paste OR sprinkled on finished mole poblano.

### Nuts (in mole architecture)
Each toasted individually before grinding — never ground raw:
- **Almond** — mole poblano, mole negro, mole almendrado. Fine, sweet richness.
- **Peanut** — mole, pipián. Earthy, rustic.
- **Walnut** — chiles en nogada (the "nogada" = walnut sauce). Sweet and tannic.
- **Piñón (pine nut)** — regional Oaxacan moles. Resinous, buttery.

### Dried fruits (in mole architecture)
- **Raisin** — mole poblano, mole negro (toasted 2–3 min). Adds prune-like sweetness.
- **Prune** — mole negro. Adds dark fruit depth.

### Chocolate/cacao
Mexican table chocolate (disk/tablet) is coarser, spiced with cinnamon, less sweet than standard. **WHEN:** added to mole at the END — stir until melted, do not boil hard. **Sub:** 70% dark chocolate + ½ tsp cinnamon per 3 oz chocolate.

### Piloncillo (unrefined cane sugar)
Molasses notes, darker than brown sugar. **WHEN:** dissolve in moles, atole, champurrado for depth. **Sub:** dark brown sugar.

### Sour orange (naranja agria)
The essential Yucatecan marinade acid. **WHEN:** in adobos and marinades (cochinita pibil, pollo pibil) — breaks down protein for tenderness while adding sour-citrus tang. **Universal sub:** ½ white vinegar + ½ orange juice (preserves both acidity and citrus fruitiness).

### Tomatillo (green husk tomato) / milpero (small wild tomatillo)
Tart, herbal, green. **USE:** salsas verdes, pipián verde. **Milpero** is smaller, more intense. **Sub:** tomatillo has no direct sub; green tomato + lime juice in a pinch.

### Nopales / prickly pear cactus
Mild, slightly slimy (okra-like), green. **USE:** grilled or boiled in salads, tacos, scrambled with eggs (nopales con huevo), in guisados. **Prepping:** trim spines and the base, dice, rinse — the slime washes off. **Sub:** green beans (texture only, not flavour).

### Huitlacoche (corn fungus / "Mexican truffle")
Earthy, mushroom-like, black. Seasonal (rainy season July–September). **USE:** in quesadillas, crepes, tamales, tacos. **Sub:** oyster mushroom + a drop of truffle oil (not the same, but closest).

### Fresh chiles (for salsas and direct cooking)
- **Poblano** — mild (1,000–2,000 SHU), rich, earthy, thick flesh. Roasted for rajas, chiles rellenos, rajas con crema. Char-peel-seed before use.
- **Jalapeño** — 2,500–8,000 SHU, bright heat. Salsas, escabeche, stuffed. The default fresh heat in most cooking.
- **Serrano** — 10,000–25,000 SHU, sharper, greener. Salsas, pico de gallo, guacamole.

### Mexican cheeses
- **Queso fresco** — crumbly, salty, mild. For topping (enmoladas, tacos, enchiladas).
- **Crema** — thick, tangy sour cream. For dolloping (tacos, sopes, enchiladas).
- **Requesón** — ricotta-like, mild, creamy. For fillings, crepes.
- **Queso Oaxaca / quesillo** — string cheese, mild, melts beautifully. For chiles rellenos, quesadillas, tlayudas.
