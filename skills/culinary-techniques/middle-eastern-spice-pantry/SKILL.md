# Middle Eastern Spice & Seasoning Pantry

The regional spices, seasonings, and pantry staples of Middle Eastern cooking (Levant through Turkey, Iran, and the Gulf) — what each does, when to add it, what it pairs with. Load this alongside `middle-eastern-cuisine-technique` for full context.

## The Pantry

### Za'atar (blend)
- **What it is**: dried wild thyme/oregano + ground sumac + toasted sesame + salt.
- **What it does**: earthy, tangy, nutty. Complex herbal-sour finish.
- **When to add**: FINISH — sprinkle over labneh, eggs, hummus, roasted vegetables, grilled meats. Never cooked into much; the base of manakish (baked on at high heat).
- **Pairs with**: olive oil, labneh, yogurt, eggs, chicken, roasted eggplant, tomatoes, flatbread.
- **Form**: dry blend (store airtight, cool/dark). Mix with olive oil ~2:1 for thick spread or 1:1 for drizzly sauce.

### Sumac (Rhus coriaria — ground dried berries)
- **What it is**: deep red, ground berries; dry, lemony sour without liquid.
- **What it does**: provides bright acid without adding moisture. Naturally slightly salty — can reduce salt.
- **When to add**: FINISH — sprinkled on fattoush, grilled chicken, marinated onions (salt + sumac + vinegar), hummus, labneh. Backbone of za'atar.
- **Pairs with**: red onion, tomato, cucumber, grilled meats, fattoush, hummus, labneh, fresh herbs.
- **Tip**: old sumac goes inert — look for red, slightly oily berries. Use liberally on finished dishes.

### Baharat (Lebanese "7 spice", also Gulf)
- **What it is**: warm/sweet (allspice, cinnamon, cloves, nutmeg) + savory (coriander, cumin) + black pepper.
- **What it does**: all-purpose aromatic seasoning — the Levantine meat workhorse.
- **When to add**: EARLY — rubbed on lamb/beef before grilling, bloomed in oil for stews and rice, folded into kofta and kibbeh.
- **Pairs with**: lamb, beef, kofta, rice, stews, stuffed vegetables, roasted potatoes.
- **Store**: airtight, up to 3 months ground. Toast whole seeds before grinding for best flavor.

### Ras el Hanout (Moroccan)
- **What it is**: warm + perfumed + earthy + slightly hot — cumin, coriander, paprika, cinnamon, ginger, turmeric, allspice, black pepper, cardamom, nutmeg, cloves, optional cayenne, rose petals, lavender, saffron.
- **What it does**: complex aromatic base for tagines, couscous, and meat rubs.
- **When to add**: EARLY — bloom in oil before adding meat/veg (rub over lamb before cooking, fold into tagines at start).
- **Pairs with**: lamb, chicken, root vegetables, chickpeas, couscous, rice.
- **Store**: airtight, cool/dark. No canonical recipe; adjust to taste.

### Aleppo Pepper (pul biber)
- **What it is**: fruity, moderately spicy (low heat by pepper standards), subtly sweet flakes.
- **What it does**: signature Turkish/Syrian finishing pepper — adds color, fruit, mild warmth, not just heat.
- **When to add**: FINISH — bloom in warm butter/oil for cilbir (poached eggs with Aleppo butter, ~1 min until bright red, don't burn); squeeze over hummus, eggs, vegetables, grilled meats.
- **Pairs with**: eggs, yogurt, hummus, chicken, lamb, roasted vegetables, olive oil, mint.
- **Substitute**: 4 parts sweet paprika + 1 part cayenne (not a perfect match but workable).

### Urfa Biber (Turkish isot pepper)
- **What it is**: dark, earthy, raisiny/red-wine-like, smoky, barely hot. A dried pepper with a unique fermented-dried character.
- **What it does**: provides deep, mellow pepper flavor and smoke without fire.
- **When to add**: FINISH — sprinkle over hummus, eggs, grilled meats as smoky contrast. Pairs well with Aleppo for heat + depth contrast.
- **Pairs with**: grilled lamb, hummus, labneh, eggs, roasted eggplant, tomato dishes.
- **Note**: its raisiny, dark-chocolate notes come from sun-drying then sweating the peppers.

### Pomegranate Molasses (dibs al-rumman)
- **What it is**: reduced tart pomegranate juice — deep ruby, sweet-tart, syrupy.
- **What it does**: provides sweet-sour balance where lemon would be too one-dimensional.
- **When to add**: MID to LATE — in dressings (fattoush, in place of vinegar), glazes (salmon, roasted vegetables), stews (Persian fesenjan — duck/walnut), marinades (with allspice for chicken), drizzled over yogurt or ice cream.
- **Pairs with**: lamb, duck, chicken, pork, walnuts (fesenjan), eggplant, roasted root veg, yogurt, fresh herbs.
- **Store**: shelf-stable after opening. Thick consistency — shake or stir before measuring.

### Dried Limes (limoo amani / loomi)
- **What it is**: whole Persian dried black limes; intense aged sourness + umami depth.
- **What it does**: souring agent that also infuses a fermented, almost mushroom-like depth unmatched by fresh citrus.
- **When to add**: MID/EARLY — PIERCE the whole limes before adding so they infuse without bursting; simmer into khoresh (ghormeh sabzi, gheimeh) 1.5–3 hr; also used whole or ground to season rice and drinks.
- **Pairs with**: lamb, beef, turmeric, fenugreek, split peas, beans, braised greens.
- **Tip**: remove seeds if bitter; some spoon out softened lime flesh before serving. Ground dried lime is also available as a spice.

### Rose Water
- **What it is**: distilled essence of rose petals; floral perfume.
- **What it does**: adds delicate floral notes to sweets and some savory dishes.
- **When to add**: OFF HEAT — a few drops in syrup (baklava), rice (advieh polo), khoresh finishes, desserts, saffron tea. Added at the very end; heat drives off the perfume.
- **Pairs with**: cardamom, pistachio, almonds, saffron, honey, yogurt, lamb.
- **Tip**: use SPARINGLY — it's potent. 1–2 tsp is enough for most dishes.

### Orange Blossom Water (mazahr)
- **What it is**: distilled essence of bitter-orange blossoms.
- **What it does**: lighter, sweeter floral than rose water. Used in pastries, fruit, some savory-sweet stews.
- **When to add**: OFF HEAT — same timing as rose water. Adds floral lift to baklava syrup, fruit salads, rice puddings.
- **Pairs with**: rose water (complement or standalone), almonds, honey, citrus, stone fruit.

### Mahlab (mahlab / mahlep)
- **What it is**: ground kernel of St. Lucie cherry stone; almond/cherry-like perfume.
- **What it does**: subtle almond-cherry fragrance in sweet breads and pastries.
- **When to add**: TO DRY INGREDIENTS — fold into flour for breads, cakes, maamoul (stuffed semolina cookies), pastries.
- **Pairs with**: cardamom, rose water, nuts, dried fruit, semolina.
- **Store**: ground mahlab loses potency relatively fast; buy whole and grind as needed if making it often.

### Mastiha (mastiha / mastic gum)
- **What it is**: piney, resinous, slightly sweet tree gum (from the mastic tree).
- **What it does**: provides stretchiness and unique flavor. Essential for Turkish dondurma (ice cream that stretches), used in baklava and some breads/stews.
- **When to add**: GROUND — a small pebble ground to powder with a bit of sugar (it's too sticky to grind alone). Add to ice cream base, pastry dough, or syrup.
- **Pairs with**: pistachio, rose water, saffron, ice cream, filo pastry.
- **Tip**: a very small amount goes a long way (1/8–1/4 tsp per batch). It's strong.

### Tahini
- **What it is**: toasted ground sesame paste; nutty, savory, rich.
- **What it does**: emulsifies with acid/ice water into sauces (hummus, tarator, dressings). Whole grain sesame butter with no added oil.
- **When to add**: EARLY in sauce building — whisk with lemon first (expect seize), then thin with water. In hummus: blended with lemon + garlic + salt first, then ice water, then warm chickpeas.
- **Pairs with**: chickpeas, garlic, lemon, yogurt, eggplant, halva (sweet tahini confection), roasted vegetables, grilled meats.
- **Tip**: stir well before measuring (oil separates on top). For a sauce: 1 part tahini : 1 part lemon+water.

### Labneh
- **What it is**: strained yogurt cheese; thick, spreadable, tangy.
- **What it does**: cooling, creamy counterpart to grilled meats, spices, and heat. Both a dip and a spread.
- **When to add**: FINISH — served as mezze dip with olive oil + za'atar, spread on flatbread, or rolled into balls cured in olive oil.
- **Pairs with**: olive oil, za'atar, Aleppo pepper, fresh mint, tomato, cucumber, grilled lamb/chicken, pita.
- **Tip**: always bring to room temp before serving for best texture and flavor. Whey that drains off is usable in soups and bread.

## Quick-Reference: When to Add

| Stage | Ingredients |
|---|---|
| **Early** (bloom / rub / cook-in) | Baharat, ras el hanout, advieh (in rice/stews), dried limes (pierced, in braises), tahini (sauce base) |
| **Mid** (braise / simmer) | Pomegranate molasses (stews, braises), dried limes (khoresh), mahlab (in dough) |
| **Finish** (sprinkle / drizzle / serve) | Za'atar, sumac, Aleppo pepper, Urfa biber, labneh, pomegranate molasses (drizzle), rose water, orange blossom water |
| **Off heat only** | Rose water, orange blossom water (heat drives off perfume) |
