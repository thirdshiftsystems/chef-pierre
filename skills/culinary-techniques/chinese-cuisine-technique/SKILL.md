# Chinese Cuisine Technique

Reusable Chinese cooking craft distilled from a roger_research deep-dive
(Serious Eats, The Woks of Life, Red House Spice, Fuchsia Dunlop). Companion
pantry file: `chinese-sauce-seasoning-pantry/SKILL.md` (a sibling sub-skill).
Sources cited at bottom.

## Core principles

- **China is not one cuisine.** Per Fuchsia Dunlop, the gap between Cantonese
  and Sichuanese food is as great as that between northern French and southern
  Italian. Technique shifts by region.
- **Home cooking is veg/grain-forward, quick, economical, little deep-frying.**
  Stir-fry, boil, and steam dominate; there is no dessert course.
- **The three arts of the Chinese kitchen** (Dunlop's training at the Sichuan
  Institute of Higher Cuisine): cutting, flavor-mixing, and the all-important
  control of heat.
- **Regional contrast at a glance:**
  - CANTONESE — freshness is doctrine. Whole steamed fish gets NO salt/seasoning/
    wine before steaming; the fish's own flavor is the dish. Signature moves:
    wok hei, velveting (oil-passing AND water-blanch), dim sum, whole steamed
    fish with ginger-scallion-soy, oyster sauce as the default umami lift.
    Seasoning light-handed: light soy, white pepper, sesame oil, scallion-ginger.
  - SICHUAN — humid-climate chili culture; málà = Sichuan-peppercorn numbing
    (má) + chili heat (là). But heat is NOT all of it: ~20 hallmark flavor
    profiles, "each dish its own style, a hundred dishes a hundred flavors"
    (yu xiang = fish-fragrant with no fish; kou shui wei; guai wei; suan ni wei;
    scorched-chili). Key techniques: gan bian (dry-fry), gan shao (dry-braise),
    shui zhu (water-poach). Doubanjiang = "the soul of Sichuan."
  - SHANGHAINESE — built on red braising (hongshao): soy + dark soy + sugar +
    rice wine into a rich reddish-brown glaze; sweet-savory; rock sugar for
    clean glossy sweetness; Chinkiang black vinegar.
  - NORTHERN — wheat country (noodles, jiaozi, bao, scallion pancakes). Dough
    craft is the core: 2:1 flour:water, medium gluten (~10-11 g protein/100 g),
    rest 30+ min until "as soft as an earlobe"; hot-water dough for
    steamed/pan-fried. Northern savory anchor: tian mian jiang (sweet bean
    sauce from fermented WHEAT flour), which Americans usually replace with
    hoisin.

## Numbered techniques (concrete parameters)

### 1. Season a carbon-steel wok
Seasoning = micro-thin oil layers polymerized into a non-stick plastic-like
coating. Remove factory coating, wash, DRY IMMEDIATELY (bare wet steel rusts).
Heat first (high burner, or 450°F/230°C oven if the handle permits) so the oil
goes on paper-thin. Oil: canola/vegetable/grapeseed — AVOID flaxseed (flakes),
butter (milk solids), olive (sediment). Buff until the pan LOOKS dry — "if you
wonder whether you used too much oil, you did" (heavy oil = sticky splotches).
Heat until smoking stops = layer done (oven ~30 min/layer); repeat until dark
brown; it blackens with use. Grace Young's trick: wash, dry, then stir-fry
Chinese chives in the new wok. Protect the patina — never let anyone scour it.

### 2. Wok heat & "hot wok, cold oil" (re guo liang you)
- Grace Young's readiness test: heat the EMPTY wok on high, flick a drop of
  water; when it evaporates immediately, the wok is ready.
- Swirl in oil; a whiff of smoke is fine. Wild smoke = overheated — cover,
  cool, wipe, wash, restart.
- The first ingredient must SIZZLE on contact. Heat the empty carbon-steel wok
  VERY hot, THEN add oil, THEN food — this prevents sticking. Never heat a
  non-stick wok empty.
- Home burners can't hold restaurant heat → BATCH everything and let the wok
  re-smoke between batches. Kenji: never double a stir-fry in one pan —
  crowding steams instead of fries.
- Best home pan: 14-inch flat-bottom carbon steel.

### 3. Aromatics + sauce-at-the-edge order
- Order: smoking oil → chilies/peppercorns bloom (~5 sec, fragrant NOT burnt)
  or ginger (~1 min) then garlic (~1 min) → protein → sauce at the END.
- Sauce goes into the CENTER and must immediately boil.
- To deglaze for wok hei, pour wine AROUND THE PERIMETER — the rim is the
  hottest zone; it sears/vaporizes instead of stewing.
- Pre-mix sauces BEFORE firing the wok — no measuring mid-cook.

### 4. Velveting (marinating) proteins
Cornstarch (+ egg white) insulates against the wok, holds moisture, and gives
the sauce something to cling to; salt/soy brine-tenderizes. Two par-cook
methods:
- A) OIL-VELVET (jau yau / zou you): sear in a lightly smoking wok — easier,
  adds a light crust that melts into the sauce.
- B) WATER-VELVET: boiling water + a little oil ~40 sec until the exterior is
  opaque but the interior is still raw; drain, shake dry. Bump seasoning if
  blanching — some washes off.
VELVET MARINADE FORMULAS (per 12-16 oz protein):
- The Woks of Life base: 2-3 Tbsp water + 2 tsp soy (or oyster) first, let
  absorb 5-10 min; THEN 2 tsp cornstarch + 2 tsp oil; rest 15-20 min (overnight
  OK).
- SE kung pao (12 oz breast, 1/2-in cubes): 2 tsp light soy + 2 tsp cornstarch
  + 1 tsp Shaoxing + pinch salt; rest 30 min.
- SE gai-lan beef (12 oz flank, 1/8-in across grain): 1/2 tsp soy + 1/2 tsp
  Shaoxing + 2 tsp oil + 1/2 tsp cornstarch + 1/2 tsp salt + 1/4 tsp sugar + 1/4
  tsp white pepper; rest 30 min.
- SE beef & broccoli (1 lb flank, 1/4-in strips): 1 Tbsp soy + 1 Tbsp Shaoxing;
  fridge 20 min-3 hr.
TIMING WHY: water needs 5-10 min to absorb BEFORE the starch; starch needs
15-30 min to hydrate and cling. CONTROVERSY (teach both): The Woks of Life call
egg white unnecessary for chicken and warn against baking soda on good meat
(soda = that crunchy takeout texture); Serious Eats' school uses egg white.
Slice meat across the grain; partial-freeze to slice thin.

### 5. Red braising (hongshao, Shanghai)
For 1.5 lb lean SKIN-ON belly cut 3/4-in pieces: parboil ~1 min until opaque
(removes impurities), rinse. Low heat: 2 Tbsp oil + 2 Tbsp rock sugar, melt,
brown pork. Low heat: 1/3 cup Shaoxing 2 min; 2 Tbsp light soy + 1 Tbsp dark
soy + 2-3 cups water (mostly cover). Cover, simmer medium 45-60 min until
fork-tender; stir every 5-10 min, top up water. Finish: uncover, high heat,
reduce to a glistening coating. Dark soy = the red-black color; skin collagen
+ sugar = the shiny sticky glaze.

### 6. Master stock (lu)
Build: 2 tsp oil + 7 slices ginger caramelize ~30 sec; 2 smashed scallions ~30
sec; 3 whole star anise + 3/4 cup rose wine + 3/4 cup Shaoxing, simmer off
alcohol; 2 cups light soy + 1/2 cup dark soy + 1 3/4 cups sugar + 12 cups
water; low simmer 20 min. Cook a 4 lb chicken (room temp, ~1 hr out of fridge):
slow boil (not rolling), lower in breast-up, fill cavity; 5 min medium-high,
lift + empty cavity; lazy simmer ~210°F/99°C covered ~25 min; OFF heat, covered
15-25 min until thigh hits 165°F/74°C; rest 10-15 min. KEEP the stock: strain
warm through a fine mesh (twice), freeze with 1-in headspace; reuse with fresh
ginger/scallion, top up proportionally. It deepens each use; use as base for
stir-fries and braises.

### 7. Fried rice (Woks of Life formula)
Rice: long-grain jasmine ideal (less starch = separate grains); day-old COLD
rice preferred (dried surface = separate firm grains). Fresh rice: rinse 2-3x,
cook with ~10% LESS water, spread on a sheet pan to cool. Batch for a 14-in
wok: 4.5-5 cups rice, 3-4 cups veg (small dice), 6-8 oz cooked protein, 2 eggs.
ORDER: wok smoking → oil → eggs barely set, out → raw protein seared ~90%, out
→ aromatics + long-cook veg 2-3 min → high heat, fatty protein 30-60 sec → rice
~3 min, flatten + scoop from bottom → seasoning 2-3 min until steam rises →
proteins/eggs/quick veg 30 sec → scallions, gather rice to center, let sides
reheat 20 sec → 2 tsp Shaoxing around the PERIMETER, toss 20-30 sec (wok hei).
Fixes: add water to clumps; 1-2 Tbsp hot stock if dry; small batches when
scaling.

### 8. Wok-fried noodles (lo mein)
Par-cook — DON'T fully boil. Fresh lo mein in salted boiling water ~3 min to
al dente, drain, run under COLD WATER until chilled (stops carryover + rinses
surface starch so noodles don't clump), drain well. Some brands sell
pre-boiled ready-to-fry. Then: smoking wok → oil → noodles 1 min (chopsticks to
separate clumps) → sauce → 1 min → everything back → 1 min.

### 9. Knife cuts (Serious Eats / Lucas Sin)
- Roll cut: 45° slice, quarter-turn rotate — max surface area for sauce, even
  pieces that toss well.
- Julienne/strip cut: ~2mm x 2mm (planks → stack → cut). Dice = strips cut
  crosswise.
- Bias slice: 45° against the grain for meat/seafood stir-fry.
- Horizontal slice: on partially-frozen meat, saw — don't force.
- Smash garlic/ginger for maximum aromatic release.
- WHY SIZE MATTERS: cut all main ingredients to roughly the same size so they
  cook evenly — in a 60-second stir-fry, consistent thickness = consistent
  doneness.

### 10. Dim sum — har gow (translucent shrimp dumpling)
Dough: 1 cup WHEAT STARCH + 1/2 cup cornstarch (or tapioca) = 2:1 starch ratio
+ 1 1/4 cups BOILING water + 3 tsp lard. The water MUST be boiling — it
gelatinizes the starches into translucent pliable dough. ~18 pieces, ~22 g each.
Keep covered under a DAMP towel (it dries and cracks in minutes); roll 3-in
circles with thinner edges. Filling (1/2 lb shrimp): 1 tsp oyster + 1 Tbsp oil
+ 1/4 tsp white pepper + 1 tsp sesame oil + 1/4 tsp salt + 1 tsp sugar + 1/2 tsp
ginger, whipped ONE direction until sticky + chopped bamboo shoots. Steam 6 min
HIGH heat, 1.5 in apart; serve immediately (does not reheat).

### 11. Dim sum — siu mai
Filling is an EMULSION: 1 lb pork + 8 oz shrimp + 1 Tbsp cornstarch + 1 Tbsp
Shaoxing + 1 tsp salt + 1/4 tsp white pepper + 1 Tbsp oil + 1 Tbsp oyster + 1/2
tsp sesame oil; mix in a stand mixer ~5 min (10-15 min by hand, ONE direction)
until paste-like and sticky — that's the "snap." Shrimp trick: 1 tsp sugar + 1/8
tsp BAKING SODA + 2 Tbsp water, rest 15 min, rinse until clear (alkaline
firming = crunch; the legitimate soda use in dim sum). Thin HK round wrappers;
1 Tbsp filling, open top; steam medium ~9 min (water at the steamer rim ~1/2
in). Freeze raw, steam from frozen +1-2 min.

### 12. Char siu glaze
Per 2 lb dark meat: 2 Tbsp dark brown sugar + 1 tsp salt + 1/2 tsp five-spice +
1/4 tsp white pepper + 1.5 Tbsp hoisin + 1 Tbsp Shaoxing + 2 tsp light soy +
1/2 tsp sesame oil + 2 cloves garlic (5 drops red coloring optional). Marinate
8 hr-overnight; roast 375°F/190°C: 30 min (check every 15 — sugar scorches),
baste with marinade simmered with 1 Tbsp maltose/honey, 5-10 min more to
165°F/74°C (breast: pull at 160°F); final baste + 1 min for the charred crust.
Char = sugar + heat + basting, not flame.

### 13. Whole steamed fish, Cantonese
Use a 1-2 lb whole white fish (striped bass, sea bass, sea bream, grouper,
flounder), head + tail on. The Woks of Life: NO salt/seasoning/wine before
steaming. Red House Spice: pat dry, 45° slashes both sides, ginger + scallion
under/over/in the cavity. TIMING: 400 g/14 oz = 8 min HIGH heat (RHS; +2 min
if larger); 1.5 lb = 9 min (WOL). Start the timer the moment it goes in; the
water must be boiling. DONENESS: meat opaque to the bone, bone still slightly
translucent (WOL); flesh lifts off the bones with a chopstick poke (RHS). POUR
OFF all steaming liquid (cloudy condensation dilutes the sauce).

### 14. Sichuan dry-fry (gan bian)
Dehydrate the ingredient so the dish looks dry and takes pungent spices.
Restaurants deep-fry; at home: coat 12 oz (350 g) green beans with 1 Tbsp oil +
1/4 tsp salt (bag-shake), then oven 425°F/220°C 12-15 min (fan 200°C), or
air-fry 390°F/200°C 6-8 min (shake twice), or wok medium-high 12-15 min — until
PLIABLE and BLISTERED. Then fast stir-fry: hot wok, oil, 1 tsp whole Sichuan
peppercorns + 6 dried chilies till fragrant (don't burn), minced meat + 1 tsp
Shaoxing till pale, 1.5 Tbsp ya cai + garlic + ginger + 1 tsp light soy + 1/2
tsp dark soy ~1 min, beans in, toss.

## Signature dishes

### Kung Pao chicken (Sichuan) — velvet + chile bloom + sweet-sour glaze
Marinade (12 oz breast, 1/2-in cubes): 2 tsp light soy + 2 tsp cornstarch + 1
 tsp Shaoxing + salt; rest 30 min. Glaze: 2 Tbsp Chinkiang vinegar + 1 Tbsp
honey + 1 Tbsp Shaoxing + 2 tsp light soy + 1/2 tsp cornstarch + 1/4 cup
water/stock. Cook: wok smoking → oil → 6-12 dried chilies (1/2-in, seeded) + 1
tsp peppercorn husks ~5 sec → chicken 45 sec-1.5 min till no pink exterior →
garlic + ginger 10 sec → scallion whites + peanuts 30 sec → glaze ~1 min till
glossy, loosen with water 1 Tbsp at a time. Doneness: chicken just cooked, glossy
coat, never gummy. Don't double the pan.

### Cantonese beef & broccoli / gai lan — batch stir-fry at max heat
1 lb flank sliced 1/4-in across grain; marinate 1 Tbsp soy + 1 Tbsp Shaoxing,
20 min-3 hr. Sauce: 3 Tbsp light soy + 2 tsp cornstarch + 3 Tbsp Shaoxing + 1/3
cup stock + 1/4 cup oyster + 1 Tbsp sugar + 1 tsp sesame oil. Cook BATCHED:
smoking wok, half the beef, don't touch ~1 min (the sear), toss till charred but
pink in spots, out; repeat; broccoli 30 sec till lightly charred (gai lan:
blanch first — 1 min regular, 30-40 sec baby); aromatics 30 sec; everything
back, sauce into the CENTER — it must boil immediately — 30-45 sec till lightly
thickened. Cues: seared edges / pink center pre-sauce; crisp-tender bright green
broccoli; sauce coats, doesn't pool.

### Mapo tofu (Sichuan) — doubanjiang bloom + braise + double-slurry
600 g soft/medium-firm tofu, 1-in cubes; blanch in salted water (1 tsp), simmer
1 min (firms cubes so they don't shatter). Hot empty wok → 2 Tbsp oil → 100 g
minced beef + 1 tsp ginger high heat till pale → 2 Tbsp Pixian doubanjiang + 1
Tbsp fermented black beans + 1 Tbsp ground chili + 1 Tbsp garlic, fry till
fragrant → 1 Tbsp Shaoxing + 400 ml water/stock, boil → tofu, boil uncovered 5
min → thicken: 2 Tbsp cornstarch + 3 Tbsp water, add HALF, wait 10 sec, add
rest; stop when sauce coats the spatula (it thickens more on the way to table —
over-thickened = lumpy) → 1/2 tsp ground Sichuan pepper + scallions, gentle mix,
OFF heat. Serve scalding hot — 烫 (hot) is one of the five required traits
(spicy, numbing, aromatic, tender, hot). WOL timing fix: ground peppercorns go
in AFTER the pork (15-30 sec), never burned; finish with fresh-ground garnish.

## Pitfalls
- Crowded wok = steamed gray food. Batch, single layers, let it re-smoke.
- Wild smoke = overheated wok; restart. Never heat non-stick empty.
- Ground Sichuan peppercorn fried in oil = bitter; add it late or as garnish.
- Over-thickened mapo slurry (adds more on the way to table) = lumpy.
- Noodles fully boiled then wok-fried = mushy clumps; par-cook + cold rinse.
- Washing a seasoned wok with soap/scouring = strips the patina.
