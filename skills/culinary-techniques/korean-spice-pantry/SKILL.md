# Korean Pantry — Spices & Seasonings

Load when building a Korean dish or shopping the pantry. Companion technique reference: `skills/culinary-techniques/korean-cuisine-technique/SKILL.md`.

## The rule of the Korean pantry: layer the three jangs

The Korean kitchen runs on three fermented pastes — **gochujang** (hot-sweet chilli paste), **doenjang** (savoury soybean paste), **ganjang** (soy sauce) — plus **gochugaru** (chilli flakes, the raw ingredient that goes INTO gochujang but used separately too), **chamgireum** (toasted sesame oil), and **garlic/perilla/green onion** as the aromatic layer. Every seasoning belongs to one role: **bloom early** (doenjang in fat), **dissolve with liquid** (gochujang in broth), or **finish off-heat** (sesame oil, sesame seeds, scallion).

## The paste & flake foundation

| Item | What it IS | What it does / when to add | Cooking rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gochujang** (고추장) | Fermented chilli paste: gochugaru + glutinous rice + meju (soybean powder) + salt, aged months–years. Thick, brick-red, glossy. | Sweet-hot backbone of the savoury side. Think: Korean BBQ marinade base, bibimbap dollop, jjigae body, tteokbokki sauce, ssamjang partner. | Add WITH liquid — dissolve in broth/water. NEVER sear on dry hot pan (scorches bitter). Bloom at medium if frying, with liquid already nearby. |
| **Gochugaru** (고춧가루) | Dried, deseeded chilli flakes — coarse or fine. NOT fermented. Mildly fruity-sweet, smokier than gochujang. | Heat + colour + mild fruity smoke. NOT interchangeable with gochujang. Coarse for kimchi (rub into salted veg); fine for seasoning/spice blends. | Toast lightly or bloom in fat for aroma; add late enough it doesn't burn. In kimchi: rub into salted vegetables FIRST, then add wet seasonings — never dump dry into liquid. |
| **Doenjang** (된장) | Fermented soybean paste — "Korean miso but stronger/earthier." Rich, funky, savoury, salty, no heat. | Stew base (doenjang jjigae), ssamjang's savoury half. Makes everything deeper and more savoury. | BLOOM IN HOT FAT / WITH MEAT EARLY. Sauté 3–4 min in oil before adding liquid — this activates the aroma. Don't simmer at hard rolling boil for the whole duration. |
| **Ganjang** (간장) | Korean soy sauce. Three grades: regular (seasoning), gukganjang (soup soy — lighter, less sweet), joseon-ganjang (dark, molassesy, long-aged). | Primary SALTY seasoning. Gukganjang for broths/soups (salt with clarity); regular for marinades; joseon for dipping / finish. | Add to taste, usually at the end or mid-cook. Soup soy goes into the broth early; regular soy in marinade; joseon as a table condiment. |
| **Ssamjang** (쌈장) | Wrap paste — a blend, don't buy bottled (easy from pantry). | The bond that makes ssam (wraps) work. | **2:1 ratio:** 2 tbsp doenjang : 1 tbsp gochujang + 1 tsp garlic + ½ tbsp sugar + 2 tsp sesame oil + 1 tsp seeds + water to thin. Stir, don't cook (though a cooked tuna/meat version exists). |

## Oils & finish aromatics

| Item | What it IS | What it does / when to add | Cooking rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chamgireum** (참기름) | Toasted sesame oil — dark, deeply nutty, Korean cooking's signature fat. | EVERY dish's finish line. Flavours: bibimbap, japchae, muchim, jjigae drizzle, ssamjang. | **ALWAYS a finish — NEVER a high-heat cooking oil.** Scorches and turns bitter. Drizzle at the very end or off-heat. |
| **Toasted sesame seeds** | *Kkae* — white or black. | Garnish + texture in japchae, bibimbap, muchim, ssamjang. | Toast dry in a pan until fragrant (30–60 sec). Add as garnish or into ssamjang. |
| **Perilla / kkaennip** (깻잎) | Minty-piney herb leaf. Fresh (leaf) + oil + powder forms. | Fresh leaf = the ssam wrap leaf (stronger/earthier than lettuce). Oil = richer version of sesame oil but different flavour profile. | Fresh: ssam + kkaennip kimchi. Oil/powder: seasoning in muchim or marinade — never high heat. |

## Stock & building blocks

| Item | What it IS | What it does / when to add | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dasima** (다시마) | Dried kelp (kombu-style). | The savoury-stock base. Boil 15 min in water for tteokbokki broth, jjigae base, or soup stock. | Don't boil too long (can turn bitter). Fish out before serving. Pair with myeolchi. |
| **Myeolchi** (멸치) | Dried anchovies, heads/guts removed. | Deep savoury-umami stock base. | Boil 15 min with dasima, then discard. The tteokbokki stock combo is 7 anchovies + 6×8-inch kelp in 4 cups water. |
| **Korean rice vinegar / cho** | Rice/spirit vinegar, often diluted to ~4–5% acidity. | The SOUR axis in the 5-flavour map. | Splash to finish jjigae (brightens doenjang). In marinades for tang. |
| **Fish sauce (myeolchi aekjeot)** | Anchovy-based Korean fish sauce. | Salty umami in kimchi paste. Standard kimchi ingredient. | Can sub with Thai fish sauce if needed (slightly different flavour). |
| **Saeujeot** (새우젓) | Salted fermented shrimp. | The classic kimchi depth ingredient — adds marine umami that fish sauce alone doesn't give. | Standard kimchi paste component (~3 tbsp per recipe). Also in some jjigae. |
| **Rice-rinse water (ssalddeumul)** | Water from the 2nd–3rd rinse of rice (not the first, which is cloudy-dirty). | Thickens jjigae broth naturally. Starchy, light body. | Use in kimchi jjigae and doenjang jjigae for a silkier texture. Don't use the first rinse. |

## Sweeteners & cheong (syrups)

| Item | What it IS | What it does | Household note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sugar / honey / oligodang** (올리고당) | Standard sweeteners. | Balances heat (gochujang) and sour (kimchi). Sugar is the default in most marinades and ssamjang. | Fine for most diets; check `kb/house_rules.md` for any restriction. |
| **Maesilcheong** (매실청) | Green plum syrup — plums macerated in sugar for months. | Sweetener with fruit-acid lift. Sub for some sugar in galbi and kimchi. | Fruit-based sweetener — flag for any fruit-restricted diet; sub extra sugar + a splash of rice vinegar in marinades. |
| **Yuzu cheong** (유자청) | Yuzu citrus syrup — yuzu macerated in sugar. | Sweet citrus lift in marinades, teas, glazes. | Citrus-based — fine for citrus-permitting diets; only non-citrus fruits are a common restriction point. |

## Marinade & enzyme system

| Item | What it IS | What it does | Rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Asian pear / bae** (배) | Grated pear, the traditional tenderiser. | Gentle enzymatic tenderiser (protease) for bulgogi/galbi. Also sweetens. | Grate, add to marinade. Safe in small amounts. |
| **Kiwi** | Strong protease fruit. | MUCH stronger enzyme than pear. | **½ kiwi max per 2 lbs meat.** Skip fruit entirely for overnight thin-cut marinades. |
| **Onion, grated** | Grated yellow/white onion. | Adds sweetness + mild tenderising effect (sulphur compounds). | Standard in bulgogi/galbi marinade. Fine for most diets (check house rules for allium restrictions). |
| **Garlic** | Minced garlic, the Korean kitchen workhorse. | Most-used aromatic — goes into nearly every marinade, stew, banchan, ssamjang. | Fine for most diets. Rule: ~2 tbsp per 450g beef in marinade; 1 tsp minced in ssamjang; garlic+ginger in kimchi paste. |
| **Ginger** | Fresh ginger, minced/grated. | Warmer, less in-your-face than garlic; traditional in kimchi paste, also in some marinades. | Typically ½–1 tsp per batch of kimchi paste. |

## Vinegar & pickling

| Item | What it IS | When used |
|---|---|---|
| **Korean pickled radish (danmuji)** | Bright-yellow sweet-pickled radish, the standard banchan + ssam ingredient. | Ssam wrapper (paired with pork belly), bibimbap topping, banchan side. The crunch + sweet-tang contrast. |
| **Quick pickle brine** | 1:1:1 vinegar:sugar:water for a quick danmuji or cucumber pickle. | Muchim-style: boil brine, pour over sliced veg, cool. |

## Shopping notes

- **Gochujang** and **doenjang** are the two must-buys for Korean cooking — everything else has a sub or is one-time. Hmart or a well-stocked grocery's international aisle. Doenjang keeps in the fridge for months; gochujang even longer.
- **Gochugaru** — coarse for kimchi, fine for general seasoning. One bag of coarse does both (crush for fine). Keep sealed, store in a cool dark place.
- **Perilla leaves (kkaennip)** — not always stocked at mainstream grocery stores; try Hmart or an Asian grocery. Fresh coriander is NOT a sub (different flavour family). If unavailable, skip — the dish still works with lettuce-only wraps.
- **Asian pear**: in the produce aisle of most grocery stores seasonally; sub a green apple (Granny Smith) + a pinch of sugar for the enzyme.
- **Saeujeot (salted shrimp)** — jarred, shelf-stable in the Korean aisle. One jar lasts through several kimchi batches.
- **Dasima (kelp) + myeolchi (anchovy)** — sold in bags in the Asian section; keeps for months in pantry. The tteokbokki stock base.
- **Check `kb/house_rules.md` for the household's dietary restrictions** (fruit-based
  sweeteners and alliums are common restriction points) — everything else in this
  pantry is broadly safe (garlic, onion, gochugaru, gochujang, doenjang, soy, sesame, vinegar, etc.).
