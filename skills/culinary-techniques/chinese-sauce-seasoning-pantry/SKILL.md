# Chinese Sauce & Seasoning Pantry

What each Chinese regional ingredient does and when in the cook it goes in. Use
with `chinese-cuisine-technique` for technique. Sources cited at bottom.

Key: **[HARD]** = hard to substitute. **[SWAP]** = workable substitute. **[TIMING]** =
when in the cook. Chinese soy names: light = 生抽 shengchou, dark = 老抽 laochou.

## Soy sauces — the salt + color backbone

- **Light soy (shengchou)** — the DEFAULT "soy sauce": thin, salty, savory.
  Chinese brands (Pearl River Bridge, LKK); Kikkoman tastes different.
  "Light" = COLOR, not low-sodium. [SWAP — in a pinch Japanese shoyu, but
  flavor differs] [TIMING: marinades, stir-fry sauces, braises, dips — early to
  mid; ~2 tsp per 12 oz protein, 2-4 Tbsp per lb in sauces]
- **Dark soy (laochou)** — thicker, darker, slightly sweet, caramelized — COLOR
  more than salt. Mushroom dark soy = extra glutamate; double-black = molasses
  depth. [HARD if you want the red-brown color] [TIMING: small amounts —
  hongshao, fried rice ("a tiny drizzle stains a wok full of rice"), noodle
  sauces. Light:dark typically 2:1.]

## Vinegar

- **Chinkiang / Zhenjiang black vinegar** — dark, malty, complex, faintly sweet.
  [SWAP — balsamic closest] [TIMING: dipping (xiao long bao), cold noodles,
  dressings, braised meat/fish, some stir-fries; the sour backbone of kung pao
  (2 Tbsp)].

## Wine

- **Shaoxing wine** — yellow rice wine (huangjiu), ~14-15% ABV, dark amber,
  mellow sweet; cooking bottles are salted. [SWAP — dry sherry best, other rice
  wines, dry white; non-alc: stock. Mirin works but sweeter — cut sugar]
  [TIMING: marinades (1-2 tsp), dumpling fillings, deglaze (1-2 Tbsp), braises
  in volume (1/3-3/4 cup); blanch gamey meat with ginger + Shaoxing].

## Fermented & condiment sauces

- **Oyster sauce** — oyster extract, umami bomb, NOT fishy. "If you want any
  dish to taste better, just add oyster sauce." [SWAP — vegetarian "stir-fry
  sauce"] [TIMING: sauces, marinades, braises; a little goes a long way].
- **Doubanjiang (chili bean paste, Pixian best)** — fermented broad beans +
  chilies; "the soul of Sichuan." Chop a regular version finer. Salty — adjust.
  [NOT sweet bean paste / tian mian jiang — different product] [TIMING:
  bloomed in hot oil EARLY (fried until fragrant) before any liquid — mapo,
  twice-cooked pork, shui zhu].
- **Hoisin** — fermented bean paste + garlic/chili/sesame, sweet-thick. [SWAP —
  tian mian jiang + a little soy + sugar] [TIMING: marinades/BBQ (char siu);
  Peking duck in the US (China uses tian mian jiang)].
- **Tian mian jiang (sweet bean sauce)** — fermented WHEAT flour; the northern
  savory anchor (Peking duck, zha jiang mian). Americans usually sub hoisin.

## Finishing oils & umami

- **Toasted sesame oil** — FINISHING oil, not cooking oil. [TIMING: last 30 sec
  or off-heat (mapo: sesame oil + scallions at the very end); cold dips]. A few
  drops — it dominates (even a little can distract in kung pao).
- **MSG** — pure umami; the "debate" is cultural — glutamates already arrive via
  mushroom dark soy, oyster sauce, seasoned soy. [TIMING: in the seasoning mix
  (fried rice: 1/2 tsp optional) or sprinkle at the end].
- **Fermented black beans (douchi)** — salty, earthy umami; mapo, black-bean
  sauces. Rinse/rough-chop before use.
- **Ya cai** — Sichuan preserved mustard stem; dry-fried green beans.

## Spices & seasonings

- **White pepper** — fine, musty heat — the stir-fry pepper. [SWAP — white NOT
  black: black reads differently] [TIMING: marinades (1/4 tsp scale), sauces,
  steamed-fish dressing, finish].
- **Sichuan peppercorn** — NOT a pepper: prickly-ash husk; lemony, numbing via
  hydroxy-alpha-sanshool (the "9-volt battery" tingle). Flavor is in the HUSKS —
  discard stems/seeds. [HARD for mala] [TO NUMB, NOT BURN: bloom whole husks in
  oil 5-15 sec for fragrance; dry-toast ~30 sec for aroma; grind fresh and add
  LATE or as garnish for max numbing; NEVER fry ground peppercorns in oil (bitter)].
  1/2 tsp to 1.5 Tbsp per dish; quality varies hugely.
- **Dried chilies** — snip into 1-cm pieces, SHAKE OUT the seeds (seeds = heat
  without flavor). [TIMING: bloomed in oil at the START (~5 sec, fragrant not
  burnt) to flavor the oil — scorched-chili technique; whole for braises].
- **Five-spice** — star-anise-forward blend; char siu, braises. Use sparingly.
- **Sugar / rock sugar** — balances salt, heat, vinegar. Rock sugar dissolves
  slow — cleaner, glossier sweetness for braises (hongshao: 2 Tbsp per 1.5 lb);
  granulated for fast sauces; kung pao uses honey.

## Formulas to memorize

- Universal stir-fry sauce (gai lan): 2 Tbsp water + 1 tsp sesame oil + 2 tsp
  oyster + 1 tsp light soy + 1 tsp cornstarch.
- Rich stir-fry sauce (beef & broccoli, per 1 lb beef + 1 lb broccoli): 3 Tbsp
  light soy + 2 tsp cornstarch + 3 Tbsp Shaoxing + 1/3 cup stock + 1/4 cup
  oyster + 1 Tbsp sugar + 1 tsp sesame oil.
- Kung pao glaze: 2 Tbsp Chinkiang + 1 Tbsp honey + 1 Tbsp Shaoxing + 2 tsp
  light soy + 1/2 tsp cornstarch + 1/4 cup water/stock.
- Velvet marinade (per 12-16 oz): 2-3 Tbsp water + 2 tsp soy/oyster + 2 tsp
  cornstarch + 2 tsp oil; rest 15-20 min.
- Red-braise liquid (per 1.5 lb): 2 Tbsp light soy + 1 Tbsp dark soy + 2 Tbsp
  rock sugar + 1/3 cup Shaoxing + 2-3 cups water.
- Lo mein noodle sauce (per 1 lb): 1 Tbsp sesame oil + 2 tsp light soy + 2 tsp
  dark soy + 1 Tbsp oyster + 1/4 tsp salt + 1/4 tsp sugar + 1 tsp Shaoxing +
  1 Tbsp water.
