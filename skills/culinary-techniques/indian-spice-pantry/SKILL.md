# Indian Pantry — Spices & Seasonings

Load when building an Indian masala or shopping the pantry for an Indian dish.
Companion technique reference:
`skills/culinary-techniques/indian-cuisine-technique/SKILL.md`.

## The rule of the pantry: layer by volatility

Three insertion points, always in this order — this is the single most important
idea in Indian seasoning:

1. **Whole spices EARLY** — in fat, before the onion. They need time and heat to
   open; frying "liberates essential oils from cells."
2. **Ground spices MID** — bloom 20–45 s in fat at 150–170 °C, with the tomato/masala.
3. **Fragile aromatics LATE / OFF-HEAT** — garam masala, kasuri methi, lemon,
   coriander leaf, kewra/rose. Heat kills them.

## Whole spices — bloom EARLY (in fat, before onion)

| Spice | What it does | When / how |
|---|---|---|
| **Cumin seed (jeera)** | Earthy base note; the default tadka seed | Splutters in 5–10 s; don't let it go black |
| **Mustard seed (rai)** | Pungency, pop; South & East signature | Pops ~180–190 °C; add before cumin's quiet |
| **Fenugreek seed (methi)** | Bitter-sweet, maple; warm depth | Burns fast — watch it closely |
| **Black cardamom (badi elaichi)** | Smoky, camphor; heavy meat gravies, biryani | Whole, early; huge aroma |
| **Green cardamom, clove, cinnamon/cassia, black pepper, bay/tej patta, star anise, mace (javitri), nutmeg** | The garam-masala family | Roasted then ground, or whole into rice/biryani |
| **Dried red chilli** | Smoke + pungency in a tadka | Early in the tempering fat |
| **Curry leaves** | South Indian/Ceylon identity; citrusy-fresh | Fry 10–20 s, pat dry first, late in the tadka |
| **Radhuni (wild celery seed)** | Bengali alternative to mustard in panch phoron | Whole, bloomed in mustard oil |

## Ground spices — bloom MID (with tomato/masala, 20–45 s)

| Spice | What it does | When / how |
|---|---|---|
| **Turmeric (haldi)** | Colour, earthiness, preservative | Scorch-prone — add as a water slurry or with tomatoes |
| **Coriander (dhania)** | The bulk/body spice, sweet-citrus | Highest-volume ground spice in most masalas |
| **Red chilli powder** | Heat; Kashmiri for colour without much heat | In a tadka: ⅓ tsp per 4 servings, then off heat |
| **Cumin powder** | Earthy depth in blends | With the ground masala |
| **Asafoetida / hing** | Allium substitute (onion/garlic note); sulphur | Late in the fat; ⅛ tsp is a lot |

## Finish LATE / OFF-HEAT

| Spice | What it does | When / how |
|---|---|---|
| **Garam masala** | "Hot spices"; the finishing warmth | Composition varies by region — no version is more authentic; add last 2 min or off heat |
| **Kasuri methi** | Irreplaceable North Indian aroma (dried fenugreek leaf) | Crush between palms, ~1 tbsp; **no substitute** |
| **Chaat masala** | Amchur + black salt + cumin; tangy | On finished food only |
| **Amchur (dry mango), tamarind, lemon** | The acid axis | Off heat |
| **Jaggery** | Rounds out tang; Gujarati/Bengali | Off heat or late |
| **Saffron, kewra/rose water** | Biryani finish | Saffron bloomed in 2 tbsp warm milk 10 min |
| **Black salt (kala namak)** | Sulphurous; chaat, raitas | Finish |

## Regional blends

- **Garam masala** — cardamom, cinnamon, clove, cumin, coriander, pepper, bay,
  fennel, mace, nutmeg, chilli. Composition varies; roast then grind.
- **Panch phoron** (Bengali/Odia/Bihar five-spice) — fenugreek, nigella, cumin,
  black mustard (or radhuni), fennel, **equal parts WHOLE**. Always used whole,
  always bloomed in mustard oil.
- **Sambar / rasam powder** (South Indian) — coriander, toor dal, chana dal, dried
  chilli, fenugreek, curry leaf, asafoetida, pepper.
- **Tandoori masala** — coriander, cumin, paprika/Kashmiri chilli, turmeric, garam
  masala, fenugreek, ginger, garlic.
- **Gujarati profile** — mustard + cumin + hing + curry leaf + jaggery + tamarind;
  sweeter, less chilli.
- **Hyderabadi profile** — heavier on black cardamom, mace, saffron, coconut,
  tamarind — the biryani axis.

## Shopping notes

- **Check `kb/house_rules.md` for the household's dietary restrictions** before
  relying on any ingredient below (fruits, sweeteners, dairy and aromatics are
  common restriction points).
- Curry-heavy dinners may be flagged for lingering kitchen odour — bank the
  technique, but don't make Indian a default weeknight; save it for when the
  house is ready for the aroma.
- Curry leaves freeze well; garam masala and ground blends are best bought fresh
  and used within ~3 months for real aroma.
