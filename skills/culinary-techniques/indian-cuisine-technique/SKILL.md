# Indian Cuisine — Technique

Load when cooking or teaching Indian dishes: dal tadka, a Punjabi/South Indian
curry, biryani, tandoori chicken, paneer dishes, sukhi sabzi, raita. Companion
pantry reference: `skills/culinary-techniques/indian-spice-pantry/SKILL.md`.

## Core principles

1. **Fat is the flavour carrier.** Nearly all spice aroma is fat-soluble. Ground
   spices dropped into water taste flat; the same amount bloomed in 150–170 °C
   ghee/oil is a different ingredient. Tempering works because frying "liberates
   essential oils from cells."
2. **Layer by volatility.** Three insertion points, always in order: **whole
   spices early** (need time + heat to open), **ground spices mid** (bloom
   20–45 s), **fragile aromatics late/off-heat** (garam masala, kasuri methi,
   lemon, coriander, kewra). Garam masala is canonically added just before the
   end of cooking.
3. **Bhuna = reduction, not just frying.** You drive off water and brown the
   onion-tomato-sugar-protein network. The cue is **oil separating** from the
   masala — the water has gone and the fat re-emerges. That is the moment it
   becomes a gravy base.
4. **Balance on five axes:** masala / sour / sweet / salt / heat. Fix a "missing"
   curry on the **acid** axis (amchur, tamarind, lemon, yogurt, tomato) before
   adding more spice.
5. **Dairy is buffer and body.** Yogurt/paneer/cream buffer chilli heat, add
   lactic tang, and emulsify. Paneer is acid-set and **non-melting** — it holds
   shape, so it goes in last and gently.
6. **Work by weight, cook by cue.** Gram-based ratios, but doneness is sensory:
   onion colour, oil separation, dal mushiness, rice bite.

## Numbered techniques (specifics)

### 1. Tadka / chaunk / vaghar / talimpu / oggarane (one technique, five names)
Ghee, medium heat, **shimmer only — not smoking**. Order: **mustard seeds →
cumin → dried red chilli → dal/hing/curry leaves → garlic last**. Cumin splutters
in 5–10 s. Garlic "fry lightly until aromatic — do not brown, it tastes bitter."
Powder goes in **off-heat** (or with a splash of water). Pour sizzling over the
finished dish at the table.
- **Fails:** burnt garlic/mustard = bitter; cold fat = no bloom; added too early =
  aromatics spent before serving.

### 2. Blooming ground spices
Add to fat at **150–170 °C for 20–45 s**. Turmeric scorches fast — add as a
slurry with a splash of water, or with the tomatoes. Red chilli powder in a tadka:
**⅓ tsp for a 4-serving dish**, added then *immediately* off heat.
- **Fails:** grey, raw-powdery taste (under-bloomed); acrid/scorched (over-bloomed).

### 3. Onion–tomato masala (bhuna) base
Onion:tomato runs **1:1 to 2:1 by volume**; start 1:1. Onion is 60–70% of the
base — fry **12–15 min medium to deep golden** before tomatoes. Tomatoes in, cook
**uncovered, stirring regularly, until it dries and thickens (~15 min)**. Oil
separation = done.
- **Fails:** raw onion bite (under-fried); pale gravy (onions not taken to golden);
  watery masala (tomatoes never reduced).

### 4. Ginger–garlic
**1:1, ~1 tbsp each** (paste or chopped) per 2–3 servings, added **after onions go
translucent**, fried **30–60 s only**. Ground/powdered ginger-garlic are **not**
substitutes for fresh in a proper masala.
- **Fails:** browned garlic (bitter); raw sharpness (not fried the 30–60 s).

### 5. Adding yogurt without curdling
Whisk smooth, ideally **hung 30 min** to drop whey; **1 tsp besan or flour per cup
of yogurt** as a stabiliser; heat **off or lowest flame**, add in a thin stream,
stir constantly **2–3 min** until it re-separates oil. Boiling yogurt = split.
- **Fails:** split/curdled (too hot, added too fast); grainy (not whisked / not hung).

### 6. Dal cookery
Standard ratio: **1 cup dal (¾ toor + ¼ moong + 2 tbsp chana) : 3 cups water :
¼ tsp turmeric : 1 tsp salt**. Pressure cook **3–4 whistles medium** (Instant Pot:
**10 min high pressure**). Rinse 4× until the water runs clear; skim scum. Pot
method: soak in hot water 30 min, rolling boil, then 20–30 min uncovered. **Old
lentils take more water** — add ½–1 cup hot as needed. Chana dal should retain
texture, not dissolve.
- **Fails:** gluey dal (too little water / over-cooked toor); crunchy chana (fine —
  intended); bland (salt added too late).

### 7. Tarka for dal (restaurant-style, North Indian)
**2 tbsp ghee · ½–¾ tsp cumin · ¼ tsp mustard (optional — N. Indian often omits)
· 2 dried red chillies · ½–1 tbsp garlic · then ⅛ tsp hing + ⅓ tsp chilli powder
off heat.** Curry leaves optional here but the **default** in South Indian versions.
Simmer the finished dal with the masala **6–7 min** to marry.
- **Fails:** tadka not simmered in (flavours sit on top); burnt garlic.

### 8. Dhungar (smoke)
Heat a lump of wood coal until red (~2 min); set it in a steel cup standing inside
the pot; pour **½ tsp ghee** on it; lid on **3–4 min**; remove. This is also how you
fake tandoor smoke at home.
- **Fails:** too long = acrid campfire; cheap briquette (use real wood charcoal).

### 9. Wet vs dry architecture
**Gravy/rasedar:** 1.5–2 cups liquid per cup of finished masala; finish runny for
rice, thick for roti. **Sukhi sabzi (dry):** no added water, 15–20 min until the
masala clings to the vegetable. Thickening: cashew/khoya/cream/dahi for richness,
besan for body, onion bhuna for natural starch-sweetness.

### 10. Rice
Aged basmati; rinse till clear; **soak 20–30 min**; drain 10 min. Dum/absorption
ratio **1 : 1.5–1.75 rice:water by volume**. Open-pot: **1:5–1:6** heavily salted
water, drain at bite. Cooked rice should **snap, not smear**.
- **Fails:** sticky (rinsed too little / not aged / wrong ratio); broken grains
  (stirred too much).

### 11. Biryani dum
Two families: **pakki** (components cooked separately, then layered) and **kacchi**
(raw meat layered with rice, pot sealed, steams in its own liquid). Par-boil rice
to **~70–75%**. Seal the lid with **dough**. Dum on **low over a tawa 20–25 min**
(or 180 °C oven). Saffron steeped in **2 tbsp warm milk 10 min**, drizzled between
layers. Biryani spice set: black cardamom, mace, nutmeg, star anise, kewra.
- **Fails:** soggy biryani (rice over-parboiled / too much water); scorched base
  (no tawa/heat diffuser); raw kacchi centre (pot not tight / heat too high).

### 12. Ghee
**500 g butter → ~380–400 g ghee.** Gentle simmer **20–30 min**: foam rises,
subsides, milk solids brown on the bottom, nutty smell. Strain through muslin.
High smoke point — takes the heat whole-spice tempering needs.
- **Fails:** scorched solids (too hot = burnt-ghee taste); cloudy (not settled/strained).

### 13. Paneer from scratch
**2 L full-fat milk to 85–90 °C, off heat.** Acid: **3–4 tbsp lemon juice or 1 tsp
citric acid in ¼ cup water**, stirred gently 1–2 min until curds separate from
**greenish whey**. Rest 5 min, strain, **rinse curds in cold water** to strip
residual acid, then press **15–30 min (soft) to 1–2 h (firm)** under 2–3 kg. Yield
**~350–400 g per 2 L**. Never hard-boil after curdling — it toughens the curd.
- **Fails:** rubbery (boiled after set / pressed too hard); sour/crumbly (excess
  acid not rinsed); won't set (ultra-pasteurised or low-fat milk).

### 14. Tandoor vs stovetop char
A real tandoor hits **480 °C** with charcoal/wood, cooking by direct heat + smoke.
Home equivalents: **oven 220–230 °C on a rack 20–25 min, then 2–3 min top-shelf
broiler**; or **cast iron 4–6 min/side**; plus **dhungar (8)** for smoke. Tandoori
chicken is a **yogurt + citrus marinade** with Kashmiri chilli/turmeric for colour,
**4–24 h refrigerated**, salt ~**1–1.5% by weight**, the acid denaturing surface
protein. Score the meat; hang the yogurt first.
- **Fails:** dry chicken (no yogurt/acid / over-cooked); no char (no broiler/coals);
  pasty marinade (yogurt not hung).

## Signature dishes — how the techniques stack

1. **Dal Tadka (North Indian, restaurant-style).** Dal (6) cooked to mush → masala
   built with onion-tomato bhuna (3) + ginger-garlic (4) + bloomed ground spice (2)
   → combined and simmered 6–7 min → kasuri methi off heat → optional dhungar (8)
   → tadka (7) poured hot at the table → lemon off heat. The whole curriculum in one
   pot.
2. **Andhra-style tomato curry (South Indian, wet).** Oil at shimmer; onion +
   plum tomato chunks caramelise **5 min** then remove leaving the oil → **bloom
   cumin + black mustard seeds (2/1)** → water + chana + moong dal, simmer 5 min →
   pureed tomato, low **15 min** → garam masala + ground cumin + sugar + turmeric,
   chunks back, **2 min** to thicken. Demonstrates cumin blooming and wet
   architecture (9).
3. **Dum biryani (Hyderabadi/Lucknowi axis).** Marinate meat 4–8 h in hung yogurt +
   ginger-garlic + chilli + whole spices → fry onions to deep brown (birista) →
   par-boil aged basmati to 70–75% → layer rice–meat–rice with saffron milk, kewra,
   ghee, mint, fried onion → seal with dough → dum 20–25 min over a tawa → rest 10
   min. Layering-by-volatility (2) at its most elaborate.

## Pitfalls (cross-cutting)

- Never brown the garlic — bitter, and it's in every tadka.
- Never let turmeric or chilli powder scorch — use a water slurry or add with tomatoes.
- Never add yogurt to a boiling pot — heat off/low, thin stream, constant stir, or it splits.
- Never judge a masala by taste alone — wait for **oil separation** to know it's done.
- Never add garam masala/kasuri methi early — they are finish-line aromatics.
- Never boil paneer or re-boil a curdled milk — it toughens.
- Never skip soaking/rinsing rice and dal — that's where clean, separate texture comes from.
