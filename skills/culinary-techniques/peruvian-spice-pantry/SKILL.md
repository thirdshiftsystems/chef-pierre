# Peruvian Spice & Seasoning Pantry

Companion to `skills/culinary-techniques/peruvian-cuisine-technique/SKILL.md`.
Use when shopping for, substituting, or explaining a Peruvian ingredient.

## The chile system (get this right or nothing else works)

- **Ají amarillo** — *Capsicum baccatum*, ripens deep golden-orange despite the
  name. **Fruity, passionfruit/mango-like, 30,000-50,000 SHU** (serrano level).
  Dried = **ají mirasol** (sun-dried, raisin-like sweetness for slow dishes). The
  "holy trinity" with garlic and red onion; backbone of ají de gallina, papa a la
  huancaína, lomo saltado, causa.
  **WHEN:** fresh strips EARLY in a stir-fry (needs brief cooking to bloom); as
  paste in sofritos and creams cooked with the aromatics; in ceviche only as
  paste in the leche de tigre (fresh strips would dominate — ceviche's chile is
  ají limo).
- **Ají panca** — *Capsicum chinense*, Peruvian coast, 3-5 in long, thick flesh,
  deep red-burgundy, sun-dried. **~100 SHU — essentially NO heat** deseeded and
  deveined; pure flavor + color: smoky, earthy, fruity.
  **WHEN:** the workhorse for marinades and braises where you want depth without
  fire. Anticuchos ½ cup paste per heart; pollo a la brasa ½ cup ground per bird;
  tacu tacu 1 tbsp; escabeche 1 tsp powder; arroz con pollo 1 tbsp sofrito.
  **Always add early and bloom in fat.**
- **Ají limo** — *Capsicum chinense*, small, intensely aromatic, citrusy/lemony
  perfume (named for Lima). **30,000-50,000 SHU (several times hotter than
  jalapeño)**, ripens green→yellow→orange→red; purple variety exists. **The fresh
  heat behind ceviche.**
  **WHEN:** fresh, finely minced, INTO the finished ceviche or salsa criolla —
  never cooked. Habanero is the accepted substitute; ají limo is the traditional
  option.
- **Rocoto** — *Capsicum pubescens* (hairy leaves, BLACK seeds — distinctive),
  thick-walled, pungent. **Heat wildly variable: 2,400-250,000 SHU.** Used
  roasted/stuffed (rocoto relleno) and in crema de rocoto; a defining chile of
  nikkei cuisine.
  **WHEN:** heat lives in the flesh and veins — roast, peel, seed, then blend
  into sauces. Gloves on.

## Herbs & aromatics

- **Huacatay** (Peruvian black mint, *Tagetes minuta*, marigold family, Quechua
  name) — minty + basil + citrus (some detect tarragon/coriander). **The soul of
  ocopa (1 tsp paste or leaves, blended)**; a whisper (½ tsp paste) in the ají
  amarillo chicken dip.
  **WHEN:** always raw/blended at the END — heat destroys its top notes; a little
  goes far.
- **Culantro (recao)** — *Eryngium foetidum*, "Mexican coriander," long serrated
  leaves, stronger and more persistent than cilantro. Heavy in Peruvian Amazonian
  cooking; on the coast, cilantro (coriander leaves) is the daily herb.
  **WHEN:** near the end or blended raw — arroz con pollo's full blended cup of
  cilantro is the coastal standard.
- **Achiote (annatto)** — seeds of *Bixa orellana*; orange-red from bixin/
  norbixin; sweet, peppery. Sold as powder, paste, or oil/lard infusion.
  **WHEN:** bloomed in fat EARLY in sofritos for color and warmth — tacu tacu:
  1 tbsp achiote paste with the onion and ají pastes.

## Sweeteners & fermented liquids

- **Chancaca** — raw unrefined sugarcane sugar (synonym for panela), often
  orange-peel/cinnamon flavored. Desserts (sopaipillas, picarones) and it aids
  fermentation of chicha de jora.
  **WHEN:** in a braise/sauce for molasses depth, or melted for dessert syrup;
  dissolves slowly — crush or simmer.
- **Chicha de jora** — prehispanic corn beer: malted maize, boiled wort,
  fermented several days in earthenware; pale straw, slightly milky/sour,
  **1-3% ABV**. The traditional braising/rice liquid — arroz con pollo's original
  recipe used it, now usually replaced by **1 cup malt beer**. If you have real
  chicha, use it exactly where the beer goes.
- **Pisco** — Peruvian grape brandy, **33-50% ABV**, clear or yellow, Cultural
  Heritage designation 1988. Base of the national cocktail **pisco sour
  (2:2:2 pisco:lime:syrup + egg white, 10-second shake)**; also maracuyá sour and
  chilcano.

## Umami & dairy (the richness engine)

- **Soy sauce** — brought by Chinese immigrants; the umami engine of chifa.
  **WHEN:** added at the **END** of lomo saltado with the vinegar so it doesn't
  scorch — the sauce forms from beef juices + soy + vinegar (**~4:3 soy:vinegar by
  volume**); and as a marinade base for pollo a la brasa (½ cup dark soy,
  overnight).
- **Evaporated milk** — the cream substitute that DEFINES Peruvian cremas
  (huancaína, ocopa, ají de gallina): body and richness without fresh cream's
  instability. **WHEN:** blended cold in huancaína/ocopa; added OFF-HEAT at the
  end in ají de gallina. **Never boil after adding.**
- **Queso fresco** — fresh white cheese, mild, milky, crumbly. The dairy tang in
  huancaína (8 oz per ~1.5 cups sauce) and ocopa (½ cup).
  **WHEN:** blended into sauces, or crumbled raw over plates. Sanctioned subs:
  cream cheese, cottage cheese, feta.

## Starches & produce

- **Choclo / Peruvian corn** — large-kernel Andean field corn (Cuzco corn;
  Quechua *choccllo*); kernels bigger, chewier, starchy, hominy-like rather than
  sweet. **WHEN:** boiled (about 10 min in water with a little sugar) as the
  ceviche/anticucho side, cut in thick slices for anticucho plates; cancha
  (toasted corn nuts) as garnish.
- **Potatoes (papa amarilla et al.)** — Peru holds the world's widest potato
  variety; the yellow/floury potato is the default for causa (Yukon Gold or
  Russet stand in — **starchy texture is key**), huancaína plates, and thick
  steak fries in lomo saltado. **WHEN:** boiled whole and peeled hot for causa;
  fried thick for lomo saltado / brasa.

## Substitution quick-panel

| Want | Don't have | Use |
|------|-----------|-----|
| ají amarillo paste | jarred too hot | taste as you go; halve the quantity, add more |
| ají limo | — | habanero |
| ají amarillo, fresh (lomo) | — | jalapeño + red bell mix |
| ají panca paste | — | you lose smoke/color; guajillo/anaheim + a little smoked paprika |
| queso fresco | — | cream cheese / cottage cheese / feta |
| evaporated milk | — | half-and-half |
| chicha de jora | — | malt beer, same volume |
| yellow potato | — | Yukon Gold or Russet (starchy) |

## Pitfalls

- **Ají panca is not spicy.** Using it for heat gives color and smoke but no kick.
- **Huacatay is pungent.** 1 tsp paste is the guide; more tastes like a marigold
  bed.
- **Peanuts must be toasted** in ocopa — untoasted = raw, dull sauce.
- **Dried chile pastes need blooming in fat**; raw powder in a fast pan tastes
  powdery and raw.
- **Never boil evaporated milk** once it's in a sauce.
- **Rocoto heat is unpredictable** — the same batch can range from mild to brutal;
  taste before committing a whole dish.
