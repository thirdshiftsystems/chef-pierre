# Thai Cuisine Technique

Reusable Thai cooking craft distilled from a roger_research deep-dive (Serious
Eats + Hot Thai Kitchen / Pailin Chongchitnant). Companion pantry file:
`thai-cuisine-technique/spice-pantry/SKILL.md`. Sources cited at bottom.

## Core principles

- **Four regional cuisines, not one style** — Central (Bangkok: sweet, lush
  coconut curries, palm sugar, Chinese-influenced stir-fries/noodles), Southern
  (fiery, funky, seafood, turmeric, budu fish sauce), Isan/Northeast (no
  coconut milk, light + grilled + pounded, pla ra/padaek, sticky-rice staple),
  Northern (forager herbs/greens, warm dried spices in khao soi, nam phrik
  dips). Technique shifts accordingly.
- **Balance hot/sour/salty/sweet + umami in EVERY dish**, cooked strong because
  it's eaten over plain rice. Never cloyingly sweet — over-sweetening abroad is
  a Western corruption, not the model.
- **Salt = fish sauce (nam pla)**, not table salt. Its fishiness "disappears"
  when used correctly.
- **Two different sours with two different jobs**: lime is bright/sharp/raw and
  goes in AFTER heat (heat kills its aroma); tamarind is rounded/fruity and
  SURVIVES long cooking.
- **Chilies in tiers**: mild chilies for flavor volume/color, spicy chilies for
  heat — never one type doing both jobs.
- **Diner fine-tunes at the table**: the four condiments (khrueang prung) are
  chilies-in-fish-sauce, green chilies in vinegar, dried red chile flakes, sugar.

## Numbered techniques (concrete parameters)

### 1. Build a curry paste — mortar order (traditional)
The order is the whole game:
1. Dried chilies + coarse salt FIRST (salt adds friction). Hydrate 30 min in hot
   water, or blitz dry in a coffee/spice grinder (Pailin's pick — near-impossible
   to grind dried chilies fine in a mortar unsoaked). Deseed to cut heat.
2. Fibrous aromatics next: lemongrass (sliced thin FIRST — the finer you start,
   the less pounding), galangal, makrut lime ZEST, cilantro root.
3. High-moisture aromatics LAST: garlic + shallots. Added early, their liquid
   kills friction and makes the hard stuff un-grindable.
4. Already-fine items at the END (chile powder, toasted ground spices, shrimp
   paste) — that stage is just mixing.
Pound UP-AND-DOWN (not circular); work the sides for wall friction. Too wet after
shallots? Add ground spices to absorb. Heavy granite mortar; marble is useless.

### 2. Curry paste — electric shortcuts
> **Household note: check `kb/pantry.md` § Appliances for whether a mortar &
> pestle is available.** If not, this section *is* the route — spice/coffee
> grinder for the dried chilies plus a powerful immersion blender (a
> Vitamix-class blender), or buy the paste. Never write "pound in a mortar" as
> the instruction unless the mortar is confirmed on hand.
> **Once a mortar is available:** it holds **~2 cups** — enough for a family-size curry
> paste or a batch of pad kra pao chili-garlic paste, in one go. Scale paste
> recipes to that ceiling and work in two rounds for anything larger.
- Coffee grinder for dry chilies/spices + POWERFUL immersion blender (Vitamix
  class) in a narrow jug on highest speed; add chile powder at the end. No
  liquid needed.
- Countertop blender: blades won't catch without liquid, and watery paste BOILS
  before it fries (fatal for coconut curries needing a sauté). Use only for
  pastes that don't get sautéed (kaeng som, nam ya).
- Food processor: DON'T — it chops but doesn't crush; cells stay intact, flavors muted.
- Store-bought red paste is legitimate (most Thais buy it). Maeploy is salty —
  cut fish sauce; Aroy-D/Namjai good; Thai Kitchen weak (double it).

### 3. "Crack" the paste in coconut cream (all coconut curries)
Boil ~1/2 cup of the recipe's coconut milk; add 3.5–5 Tbsp paste; stir to break
lumps; cook over MEDIUM-HIGH, reducing until VERY THICK and coconut oil visibly
separates. That oil break = the paste is blooming/frying in its own fat. If very
thick but no oil separates, just proceed. This is the bloom event — same job as
blooming dried spices.
- Coconut milk is an unstable oil-in-water emulsion; the break frees the frying fat.
- **Guar gum (cheap brands add it) PREVENTS the break** — you cannot crack the
  paste. Buy gum-free full-fat UHT.

### 4. Coconut milk — selection & layering
- Buy UHT cartons over cans (less heat = more flavor); fewest ingredients
  (avoid guar gum/xanthan/mono-diglycerides); FULL-FAT only (lite lists water
  first and is light in FLAVOR — fat carries flavor); never coconut "beverage".
  ~13–17% fat.
- Layering (full numbers): 1.5 cups milk total + 1 cup stock + 2 Tbsp fish sauce
  (split) + 2–3 Tbsp palm sugar. (1) crack 1/2 cup milk + paste; (2) protein
  tossed in paste; (3) remaining milk + stock + HALF fish sauce + palm sugar,
  simmer (~8 min chicken thigh); (4) veg by cook time (+6–8 min squash);
  (5) adjust salt at the END (paste brands differ in salt).
- Separation in the can is normal — warm and stir.

### 5. Wok technique & wok hei
- "Hot wok, cold oil": heat the DRY wok ripping hot, THEN add oil. Oil heated
  from cold breaks down into acrolein (burnt/acrid). Pro: longyau — swirl oil to
  coat, dump, add fresh small amount.
- Thin carbon steel (~3.5 lb for 14"). Aromatics must SIZZLE on contact — "if
  there is no sizzle, you have not heated the wok correctly; your wok is talking
  to you" (Grace Young).
- Protein: single layer, 1 full minute undisturbed before stirring. Max ~12 oz
  beef per 14" wok — more steams + grays.
- Wok hei = seasoning flavor + steam-toss + singed aerosolized fat + searing the
  sauce around the perimeter at the end. Restaurant phenomenon — don't wreck
  your kitchen chasing it.
- Stir-fry order: hot wok/cold oil → aromatics → longest-cooking ingredient →
  quick ingredients ("break the rawness" = cooked but crisp) → sauce around the
  EDGES → nuts/herbs as extras. Max 1–2 veg or you get "food court syndrome".

### 6. Aromatic timing (no burnt garlic)
Pad kra pao model: pound Thai chilies fine FIRST, then garlic + mild chilies to a
rough paste. Fry at MEDIUM-HIGH until garlic turns golden, ~2 minutes. Onion 30–60
s. Then HIGH for protein + sauce ~30 s. Kill heat, add basil, toss just to wilt.
If aromatics brown in under 30 s your heat is too high.

### 7. Fish sauce as salt — seasoning discipline
- Season in STAGES: half early, taste, finish — pastes vary wildly in salt.
- Buy on protein: ≥2 g protein/Tbsp (Red Boat 4 g, Squid/Megachef 2 g, Lucky 0 g —
  avoid). Ingredients = anchovies/water/salt/sugar only. Squid is saltier
  (1670 mg Na/Tbsp), Pailin's testing standard.
- Substitute: soy 1:1; sharper = soy + Golden Mountain/Maggi. Pla ra/budu are
  NOT nam-pla substitutes (different regional products).

### 8. Makrut (kaffir) lime leaf handling — two modes
- INFUSE (soups/curries): bruise/twist to release oils, tear big, drop in.
  Aroma releases in MINUTES — no long simmer. Pieces are NOT eaten (tough).
- EAT (salads/stir-fries/panang garnish): chiffonade VERY finely; remove the
  thick center stem first.
- Pastes: use the ZEST, not leaves. If only leaves: 2–3 torn ~3 min before done.
- "1 leaf" = one single leaf, not the double. Freeze fresh (dry first, foil-wrap).
- Substitute: regular lime zest. Regular lime leaves are NOT aromatic — don't use.

### 9. Thai chilies & heat management
- Bird's eye (phrik khi nu) = tiny, blazing. Spur (phrik chee fa) = mild/medium;
  DRIED spur is the default paste chili. Phrik haeng = small hot dried.
- Heat control = seed/pith removal + chili TYPING, not just quantity. Green
  paste: spicy green (Thai/serrano) + mild green (jalapeño) for volume without
  blowtorch heat; deseed AND depith the spicy ones. Red paste: mild dried
  (guajillo) color + spicy dried (arbol) heat, ~2:1 by weight.
- Never sub ultra-hot (habanero) — it rides roughshod over everything.

### 10. Palm sugar vs white sugar
- Palm sugar: less sweet, more complex (tart/smoke/caramel/butterscotch). Use
  where flavor is load-bearing: pad thai, som tam, massaman. 1 Tbsp packed ≈
  12–13 g (soft tub packs denser than puck).
- White sugar is fine when amount is small / dish is strongly flavored.
- Substitute: light brown sugar 1:1. All palm sold abroad is cut with granulated
  sugar — pick the least-cut by tasting.

### 11. Tamarind vs lime for sour
- Lime: bright/sharp/fresh; raw dressings; AFTER heat. NEVER bottled lime juice.
- Tamarind: rich/rounded/gently sweet sour, heat-stable — cooked dishes, often
  combined WITH lime (som tam). Use SOUR cooking tamarind, never sweet snacking
  tamarind. Thai/Vietnamese product ONLY (Indian concentrate is far more
  potent — a classic failed-pad-thai culprit). Block: soak in equal volume
  boiling water, squeeze/strain (more flavor than jarred). Paste keeps ~6 months
  fridge, freezes indefinitely.

### 12. Jasmine rice (khao hom mali)
Ratio 1 cup (185 g) rice : 1.25 cups (300 ml) water. Rinse 2x (surface starch =
 gummy). Tight lid, LOW heat, ~20 min; rest covered 5–10 min; FLUFF immediately
or grains compact. Brown jasmine 1:1.5. Fried-rice batches: rinse until clear, 1:1.

### 13. Sticky rice (khao niew) — soak + steam
- Wash first (loose starch = gummy). Use long-grain Thai glutinous (opaque
  grains), NOT sushi/short-grain.
- Cold-soak: 3 h minimum, ideal 4–12 h, room temp; drain. Steam: bamboo cone 15
  min, flip ball, +10 min; muslin-lined rack 20–30 min. Pot water ≤2 in deep;
  ≥1 in gap between water and rice.
- Hot-soak (fast): cover with freshly boiled water 1/2–1 in above rice, soak
  EXACTLY 20 min, drain, rinse cold, steam. Stays soft at room temp (use for
  serve-later/potluck).

### 14. Avoiding the classic failures
- Broken/curdled curry: gum-thickened coconut milk (blocks the crack), watery
  blender paste that boiled instead of frying, or lime added to something that
  simmers — lime goes in at the END. Tamarind is the sour that takes a long cook.
- Watery stir-fry: overcrowding = steaming. ≤12 oz protein per 14" wok, single
  layer, 1 min undisturbed. Pad thai = 2 servings per batch max; sauce in small
  doses around the perimeter so it sears, not stews.
- Burnt garlic: fry the chili-garlic paste at medium-high (~2 min golden), not
  screaming high; in curry the paste fries in coconut cream (moisture buffer).
- Over-salted curry: fish sauce in two stages, taste at the end.

## Signature dishes

### Green curry / gaeng keow wan — paste-frying + coconut layering
Green paste from fresh GREEN chilies (spicy + mild, seeded), toasted coriander +
cumin, white pepper, lemongrass bottom-half, galangal, makrut zest, cilantro
root, garlic, shallots, gapi + Thai basil leaves (mostly for color; aroma cooks
away). Cook: (1) boil 1/2 cup coconut milk; (2) 3.5–5 Tbsp paste, medium-high till
very thick + oil separates (the crack); (3) protein in paste; (4) remaining milk +
stock, HALF fish sauce + palm sugar, simmer (chicken thigh ~8 min); (5) veg by
cook time (squash 6–8 min); (6) OFF heat: finish salt, stir in Thai basil. Serve
strong over plain jasmine rice.

### Pad thai — balance + wok execution
The balance dish: sauce ≈ EQUAL parts palm sugar : tamarind : fish sauce
(2 Tbsp/40 g palm sugar : 2 Tbsp/35 g tamarind : 2 Tbsp+1 tsp/35 ml fish sauce
per 4 oz noodles) — sweet-leaning, and that's correct. Noodles: medium rice
noodles (rice flour + water only), soaked 30 min in ~110°F water, NEVER pre-boiled.
Hot wok/cold oil; fry pork first; aromatics (shallot, dried shrimp, pressed tofu,
sweet radish) ~40 s; egg AFTER aromatics (set, fold, break up); add noodles, pour
sauce around the PERIMETER + splash of water, toss ~2 min till absorbed; wilt half
chives + sprouts in, add rest + peanuts OFF heat. Small batches — 2 servings max.

### Tom yum (hot-sour shrimp soup) — sour/salty/heat via infusion
"Like making tea" — infuse, don't boil the herbs to death. Bare-simmer stock;
mushrooms ~5 min; torn+bruised makrut (6–7 leaves), lemongrass (7–8 × 1/8-in
slices), galangal (7–8 very thin slices), 2 Tbsp nam prik pao, 1/4 cup fish sauce,
4–5 crushed bird's eye; shrimp ~1 min; add 1 cup whole/evap milk (creamy var),
return to simmer, REMOVE FROM HEAT; stir in 1/4 cup fresh lime juice, adjust fish
sauce, cilantro. Rules: no dried herbs, no lemon rind/juice, no soy as fish sauce.

## Pitfalls
- Overcrowded wok → steamed gray food. Small batches, single layers.
- Lime added to anything that will simmer → flat, dead sour. Lime is off-heat.
- Guar-gum coconut milk → paste won't crack. Read the label.
- Whole foods: coconut milk, galangal, lemongrass, fresh chilies freeze well —
  never dried/powdered lemongrass in pastes.
