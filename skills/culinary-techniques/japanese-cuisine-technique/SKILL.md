# Japanese Cuisine Technique

Reusable Japanese craft distilled from a roger_research deep-dive (Just One
Cookbook / Namiko Chen, Umami Science, Umami Cart). Companion pantry file:
`japanese-dashi-seasoning-pantry/SKILL.md`. Sources cited at bottom.

## Core principles

- **Layering, not masking.** Dashi is the umami *base* that amplifies food
  rather than adding its own loud flavor — so dishes need less salt, fat, and
  sugar. Cook the base clean, then season lightly on top.
- **Restraint + doneness over seasoning.** Japanese food is about cooking
  things *just* right (simmer, never boil) and letting prep — knife cuts,
  soaking — do the flavor work.
- **Seasoning order matters** (sa-shi-su-se-so): it follows penetration speed
  and aroma fragility. Sugar penetrates slowest → added first; miso is most
  heat-fragile → added last, often off the heat.
- **Gentle heat is the rule.** Simmer (surface barely trembling), never a hard
  boil, which muddies stocks, toughens protein, and breaks delicate veg.

## Numbered techniques (concrete parameters)

1. **Awase dashi (the master stock).** 10 g kombu (~10×10 cm) + 10 g
   katsuobushi (~1 packed cup) per 800 ml–1 L water. Heat kombu on medium-low
   ~10 min to JUST below a boil, then REMOVE kombu *before* boiling (boiling
   leaches bitter alginic acid → slimy, bitter stock). Add katsuobushi, bring
   to a boil, simmer only **30 seconds**, turn off heat, let flakes sink
   ~10 min, strain. Never boil the flakes hard.
2. **Cold-brew kombu** for a brighter, cleaner stock: 2–3 h in summer, 4–5 h
   in winter, up to 12 h in the fridge — never longer (turns slimy/bitter).
3. **Kombu extraction temperature ~58–65 °C** is ideal. Kombu carries
   1,400–2,240 mg free glutamate/100 g; katsuobushi ~700 mg IMP/100 g.
   Glutamate + IMP together read as ~7–8× the umami of glutamate alone — that
   synergy is why awase (kombu + bonito) dashi is the everyday standard.
4. **Stronger dashi for oden/nimono:** up to 15 g katsuobushi per 4 cups.
5. **Ichiban vs niban dashi:** ichiban (first, gentle steep) → clear soups and
   chawanmushi; niban (re-steep the spent kombu+katsuobushi — 10 min simmer +
   5 g fresh katsuobushi) → miso soup and nimono. Two stocks from one spend.
6. **Rice — wash in COLD water only** (never warm; never a metal strainer,
   which breaks grains). Discard the first rinse immediately (starchy),
   agitate 10–15 s, rinse 1–2× until nearly clear. Soak 20–30 min minimum
   before cooking. White ratio **1:1.1–1.2** (a 180 ml cup → ~200 ml water).
7. **Stovetop rice:** medium heat to boil → low **12–13 min** → rest/steam
   **10 min**, lid never opened.
8. **Donabe rice:** medium-high **13–15 min**, then OFF heat and steam
   **20 min** with the lid on (donabe retains heat far better than metal — do
   not skip the rest). Brown rice 1:1.6–1.7 water, soak 6–12 h.
9. **Sa-shi-su-se-so seasoning order:** sake early (before sugar — tenderizes
   and deodorizes) → sugar → salt → vinegar → soy near the end (heat dulls its
   aroma) → miso LAST, ideally off-heat. Hon-mirin goes in early with sake;
   mirin-style (mirin-fu) goes in LAST for gloss.
10. **Teriyaki needs no marinade.** Sear dry skin-side hard **~5 min** while
    pressing it flat, flip, cover-steam **3 min**, then add the sauce and
    spoon-glaze until glossy (teri = luster). Sauce ≈ soy : sake : mirin :
    sugar = 2:2:2:1 (or 1:1 soy:mirin + half-part sake + sugar to taste).
11. **Sogigiri (slanted slice).** Hold the knife at ~45° and draw long, thin,
    wide shaves — gives even cooking and fast absorption. Use for chicken,
    mushrooms, fish.
12. **Rangiri (random cut).** Diagonal cut, rotate the piece ¼ turn, cut again.
    Keep pieces the same size for even cooking; the extra faces maximize
    surface area for nimono (nikujaga, oden).
13. **Sasagaki (gobo shaving).** Score 4 shallow lengthwise incisions
    (2–5 mm deep), shave toward you into thin curls, and drop immediately into
    water with a splash of vinegar to stop oxidation/bitterness.
14. **Usuzukiri / katsuramuki (paper-thin sheets)** for daikon or fish: made
    clean and unbruised with single-bevel blades (yanagiba/usuba/deba) whose
    flat-ground side keeps the cut running true (~15° on one side only).
15. **Tempura batter must be ICE-cold** and barely mixed — leave lumps (gluten
    development = heavy, oily crust). Fry immediately in oil at
    **170–180 °C (340–350 °F)**; shrimp ~2 min to pale golden. Never crowd:
    food should cover at most half the oil surface.
16. **Chawanmushi.** Egg : dashi = 1:2 to 1:2.5. Egg sets at 150–158 °F, so
    keep the custard in the **150–175 °F** window. Strain the custard through
    a fine sieve (removes chalazae + air → smooth, no holes). Steam gently
    **12–15 min on LOW**, vent the lid with a chopstick; an optional brief
    10–20 s high-steam kick at the start speeds setting.
17. **Nimono / nikujaga liquid ratio ≈ 8 dashi : 1 soy : 1 mirin : 1 sake,**
    plus sugar to taste. Simmer (never boil) with an **otoshibuta** (drop lid
    resting ON the food) ~20 min until potatoes are tender; skim foam; keep the
    broth thin and clear, not a gravy.
18. **Miso soup: never boil the miso** — dissolve it off the heat, ~1 Tbsp per
    150–200 ml dashi.
19. **Layered-pot cooking.** Add ingredients by cook time (hard root veg first,
    greens/aromatics last) so everything finishes together. The otoshibuta
    keeps food submerged and circulates liquid WITHOUT stirring — stirring
    breaks potatoes and clouds the broth.

## How techniques combine (signature dishes)

- **Chawanmushi (savory egg custard):** ichiban dashi → egg:dashi 1:2.5,
  season lightly (soy + salt + mirin), strain, pour over chicken/shrimp/
  shiitake in cups, cover, gentle-steam 12–15 min. Proves dashi purity,
  ratio discipline, and gentle-heat control.
- **Nikujaga (beef-potato simmer):** thin beef + potato/carrot in rangiri +
  onion; sear beef, layer veg, add 8:1:1:1 dashi:soy:mirin:sake + sugar;
  drop-lid otoshibuta; simmer ~20 min; skim; rest. Shows layered pot, cut
  logic, seasoning order, drop-lid technique.
- **Chicken teriyaki:** hard sear on dry skin 5 min → cover-steam 3 min →
  soy/sake/mirin/sugar glaze reduced and spooned glossy. Yaki + tare layering
  with no marinade.

## Pitfalls

- **Boiling kombu or hard-boiling dashi** → bitter, slimy stock (alginic acid).
- **Skipping the kombu-removal step** before it boils.
- **Using a warm rinse or metal strainer for rice** → broken, mushy grains.
- **Opening the lid during rice steam/rest** → uneven, sticky result.
- **Adding soy too early** (heat dulls its aroma) or miso too late-but-boiling.
- **Over-stirring nimono** instead of using otoshibuta → cloudy, broken broth.
- **Over-mixing tempura batter / warm batter** → heavy, greasy crust.
- **Scrambling chawanmushi** by steaming too hot or too long.
