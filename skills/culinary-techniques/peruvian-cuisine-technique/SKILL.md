# Peruvian Cuisine — Technique

Load when cooking or teaching Peruvian dishes: ceviche, lomo saltado, ají de
gallina, causa, huancaína/ocopa, pollo a la brasa, anticuchos, tacu tacu,
arroz con pollo, escabeche. Companion pantry reference:
`skills/culinary-techniques/peruvian-spice-pantry/SKILL.md`.

## Core principles

1. **Fusion by origin, not trend.** Pre-Columbian base (coast / Andes / Amazon),
   then Spanish viceregal (onion, garlic, frying, rice, beef), then immigrant
   layers: **chifa** (Chinese — wok, soy, stir-fry) and **nikkei** (Japanese —
   sashimi-grade fish handling, brief cures). Four staples: corn; potatoes /
   tubers; amaranth family (quinoa, kañiwa, kiwicha); legumes.
2. **"Criollo"** = the coastal Lima home-cooking baseline: ají amarillo + garlic
   + red onion + potato + lime + dairy richness. Chifa/nikkei are accelerants on
   top of it.
3. **Acid is a cooking method.** Lime/lemon citric acid denatures fish protein
   exactly as heat does — opaque, firm, no fire. Acid + ají are the two engines.
4. **Every dish carries a counterpoint.** Acid is balanced by starch + sweet
   (ceviche ALWAYS with boiled choclo + sweet potato). Starch-on-starch is
   embraced (lomo saltado must have BOTH fries and rice). Rich dairy sauces are
   cut by ají fruit and huacatay.
5. **Timing discipline is the modern doctrine.** Traditional ceviche marinated
   overnight in sour orange; post-nikkei it is mixed and eaten in 5-10 min.

## Numbered techniques (specifics)

### 1. Leche de tigre (ceviche marinade/sauce)
- **Trimmings version** per 1 kg white fish: **500 ml fresh lime juice (~2:1
  fish:juice by weight)**, 40 g fish trimmings, 30 g red or brown onion, 2 garlic
  cloves, 30 g celery, 15 g ginger, 4 cilantro stems (stems only), chile, salt,
  sugar. Blend 3-6 pulses until chunky; sieve and press hard.
- **Aromatics version (lighter):** juice of 6 limes + 1-inch smashed ginger + 2
  smashed garlic + ½ celery stalk + ¼ cup cilantro w/ stems + optional 1 tbsp ají
  amarillo paste. Bruise stems, infuse 5 min, strain. No fish trimmings —
  lighter and clearer; the trimmings version is richer and helps when limes
  aren't sweet.
- Squeeze limes AS CLOSE TO SERVING AS POSSIBLE; keep an ice cube in the juice.
- Taste and balance salt/sugar before serving.
- **Fails:** bitter (over-squeezed / squeezed early — don't milk the lime dry);
  gray-brown (not cold enough); thin and watery (no trimmings / over-strained —
  the denatured protein IS the body).

### 2. Ceviche cure — time, cut, oxidation
- **Cut:** ¼-inch slices exactly. Thinner cooks too fast; thicker and the
  outside overcooks before the middle sets. (Trimmings-style: 2 cm cubes.)
- **Time (Kenji's lime-juice pH ~2.5 timeline):** 0-2 min essentially raw;
  **5 min** exterior firm (edible start); **10-15 min IDEAL** — firm outside,
  tender moist interior; 30 min bordering over-cooked; 1 h fish starts falling
  apart; 2 h chalky and gone. Anywhere 5-30 min is edible-to-great.
- **Peruvian orthodoxy is tighter: no more than 5-10 min before serving.**
- **Classic ratio:** 1 lb fish : ½ cup citrus : 1 small red onion finely sliced :
  ¼ cup cilantro : 1-2 jalapeños (seeded) + salt/pepper. Marinate ≥5 min, fold
  occasionally, serve immediately.
- **Safety/freshness:** fish on ice at 32°F/0°C (ice top AND bottom, bottom
  shelf). Prefer flash-frozen-at-catch (kills parasites) or impeccably fresh:
  no fishy smell, flesh springs back, bright eyes, red gills. Best fish:
  semi-firm white ocean — sea bass, striped bass, grouper, sole/flounder. Avoid
  oily mackerel family, bluefish, jack, and freshwater fish.
- **Prep-ahead rule:** components can be prepped hours ahead, but NEVER combine
  fish and lime until shortly before serving.
- **Fails:** chalky/dry = over-marinated; slippery/raw = under-marinated or
  uneven cuts (cut uniform so pieces cure evenly); fishy = not fresh enough.

### 3. Ají amarillo paste from scratch
Halve fresh (or frozen) ají amarillos; **discard seeds and veins (gloves ON)**;
**simmer 5-10 min** in water; drain, cool; peel (easy after blanching); blend
with a couple tablespoons water or vegetable oil until creamy. Freeze in ice-cube
trays up to 3 months. **Milder:** blanch up to 3 times, changing water each time.
- **Fails:** coarse/gritty or over-acidic if you skip blanching and leave peels;
  scorched if you fry it hard; watery/weeping from too much water (use oil for a
  shelf-stable paste).

### 4. Ají panca paste / dried-chile paste
Ají panca is **~100 SHU — essentially no heat** when deseeded/deveined. Pure
flavor + deep burgundy color, smoky/earthy/fruity. **Add early and bloom in fat.**
Verified usage ratios: anticuchos ½ cup paste per heart; pollo a la brasa ½ cup
ground per bird; tacu tacu 1 tbsp paste; escabeche 1 tsp powder; arroz con pollo
1 tbsp sofrito. Dried-chile paste family method (verified for mirasol): boil the
dried chiles **changing the water 3 times**, peel, discard seeds, then blend.
- **Fails:** expecting heat (there is none); raw powdery panca in a quick pan
  (must bloom in fat or rehydrate); oxidized/off paste.

### 5. Huancaína sauce (blender order matters)
**½ cup ají amarillo paste · 2 tbsp vegetable oil · 1 cup evaporated milk ·
6 soda crackers · 8 oz queso fresco · salt.** Order: paste → oil + milk → process
smooth → THEN crackers + cheese + salt → process smooth. Keeps covered 2 days;
yield ~1.5 cups. Serve over boiled yellow potato slices with olives + egg.
Author-sanctioned subs: cream cheese / cottage cheese / feta for queso fresco;
half-and-half for evaporated milk.
- **Fails:** curdled (blend cold, never boil after); grainy (crackers not fully
  processed); gluey (too many crackers). Jarred paste can be much hotter than
  fresh — taste as you go.

### 6. Ocopa sauce (Arequipa — huacatay + peanut)
**4 oz mirasol chile (or 3 tbsp paste) · 2 tbsp ají amarillo · 6 oz onion ·
2 garlic cloves · 4 oz toasted peanuts · 2 oz toasted nuts · huacatay (or 1 tsp
paste) · 6 soda crackers · ½ cup evaporated milk + ½ cup milk · ½ cup queso
fresco · salt.** Roast onion + garlic; boil mirasol changing water 3×, peel,
seed; blend all with milks, cheese, crackers, huacatay. Serve over boiled yellow
potato, lettuce, hard-boiled egg, black olives.
- **Fails:** raw peanut flavor (**peanuts MUST be toasted — that's the engine**);
  muddy color (too much mirasol vs amarillo); huacatay overdose (1 tsp paste is
  the guide).

### 7. Causa limeña (potato dough)
Per 4 servings: **2.2 lb (1 kg) yellow/starchy potatoes (Yukon Gold or Russet)
· 3 tbsp ají amarillo paste · juice of 3 limes (~¼ cup / 60 ml) · 3 tbsp
vegetable oil · salt.** Boil whole unpeeled **25-30 min** fork-tender; peel WHILE
HOT, rice/mash immediately; **let steam escape 5 min**; season the WARM mash;
knead until smooth, pliable, bright yellow. Layer in a plastic-lined mold: dough /
filling / avocado / dough, press firmly. **Chill ≥20 min (up to 2 h)**; unmold.
- **Rules:** NEVER lime into boiling-hot potato (gluey) — wait until warm; if dry
  add oil drop by drop, **never water**; watery = too much lime/wet potato → fold
  in 1-2 extra boiled potatoes; too sour → more potato; sticks to mold → oil +
  longer chill; slice with a sharp wet knife, wiping between cuts.

### 8. Lomo saltado (wok discipline)
Per portion base: **1 lb (450 g) beef tenderloin / skirt / other tender
quick-cooking steak, sliced thin AGAINST the grain**; ¼ cup oil (peanut/canola);
1 medium red onion ½-inch slices; 4 scallions 2-inch lengths; 1 ají amarillo in
matchsticks; 2 plum tomatoes ¾-inch wedges; 2 garlic cloves + 1-inch ginger
minced; **1 tbsp + 1 tsp (20 ml) soy sauce : 1 tbsp (15 ml) apple cider vinegar
(~4:3 soy:vinegar is the balance point)**; 2 tsp cilantro.
- **Order & heat:** wok on HIGHEST heat until smoke curls; oil until it smokes.
  Sear beef in **small batches (2-3 on a home range)** — crowding = steaming, no
  sear. Leave pieces **30-60 s** untouched for hard char, toss, remove and KEEP
  THE JUICES (they are the sauce base). Re-smoke; sear onion to deep char but
  crisp-tender (thick cuts survive the heat), then scallions, then ají amarillo,
  then **tomatoes LAST and briefly** (never to mush); garlic + ginger at the end.
  Return everything + beef juices; add soy + vinegar last; a brown sauce forms
  from juices + soy + vinegar + oil; big pinch cilantro; out of the wok.
- **Architecture:** fries and rice are non-negotiable; fries on top, under (to
  absorb juices), or on the side.
- **Fails:** gray steamed beef (crowded/low heat); rubbery (wrong cut or overcooked
  sirloin — sirloin dries fast); mushy veg; watery sauce (no juice reduction /
  stewed tomatoes); sticky-sweet sauce (too much soy, no vinegar).

### 9. Pollo a la brasa (marinade + roast)
**Marinade per whole chicken:** ½ cup plain vinegar · ½ cup ground ají panca ·
½ cup fresh garlic paste · ½ cup dark soy · ½ cup vegetable oil · 1 cup black
beer (optional) · salt, pepper, cumin. Mix dry first, then wet; coat fully;
**marinate overnight (12 h) in the fridge.** Roast: re-spread marinade; baking
pan with **1 inch of water in the bottom**; **390°F / 200°C**, roast **~2 h**
until juices run clear at the thigh joint and skin is crisply roasted. Start
fries ~30 min before it comes out. Traditional method is charcoal rotisserie
(turned by hand ~1 h) — the water bath is the modern oven safeguard.
- Ají amarillo dip (same source): 1 large ají amarillo (or 2 tbsp paste) · ⅓ cup
  queso fresco · ¼ cup oil · 1 garlic clove · 3 tbsp lime · ½ tsp huacatay paste
  · pinch sugar · pepper — food processor, seconds.
- **Fails:** dry chicken (no water pan / too long high heat); raw center with
  burnt skin (check the THIGH JOINT, not just breast); pasty skin (marinade not
  patted, or sugary marinade); scorched garlic.

### 10. Anticuchos (beef heart skewers)
Clean the heart thoroughly — remove fat, veins, sinew (halve it first); cut into
**1½-inch squares**. **Marinade: ½ cup ají panca paste · 1 tbsp garlic · ¼ cup
red wine vinegar · 1 tbsp dried oregano · ½ cup vegetable oil · 1 tsp cumin ·
salt. Marinate ≥3 h.** 3-4 pieces per soaked bamboo skewer. **Grill MEDIUM heat,
basting with the marinade using a corn-husk brush, ~3 min per side** until cooked
through. Serve with boiled potato slices, boiled choclo slices, and ají amarillo
paste mixed with chopped scallions + salt.
- **Char vs cook-through:** heart is dense and lean — medium heat + aggressive
  basting keeps it moist while it cooks through; high heat chars the outside
  before the center is done.
- **Fails:** shoe-leather (too-high heat or sinew not trimmed — **cleaning is 80%
  of the dish**); raw center (heat too low / crowded); bitter char (marinade
  burning — move to a cooler zone).

### 11. Arroz con pollo peruano (green cilantro rice)
**½ cup olive oil · 4 chicken legs · ½ red onion · 1 tbsp garlic paste · ¼ tsp
cumin · 1 tbsp ají amarillo paste · 1 cup blended fresh cilantro leaves · 1 tbsp
ají panca paste · 2 cups white long-grain rice · 2½ cups stock (or 2 cups stock
+ 1 cup malt beer) · ½ cup cooked peas · ½ cup cooked diced carrot.**
Method: season + fry chicken legs to seal (not cooked through), remove. Same pot:
fry onion, garlic, both ají pastes, cumin ~2 min medium; add cilantro paste, cook
~5 min more until onion soft; return chicken, cook ~20 min until fully cooked;
remove. Add rice to the seasoned pot + the liquid; **mix so EVERY grain is
coated** (else the rice won't take the green); boil UNCOVERED, cover, low **15
min**; rest.
- Green = Chiclayo/north-coast (original used chicha de jora, now usually malt
  beer). Red = ají panca. Yellow = turmeric.
- **Fails:** white patchy rice (not mixed through the liquid); mushy rice (the
  2½ cups per 2 cups rice is the ceiling, INCLUDING any beer); scorched bottom;
  chicken underdone (check thigh).

### 12. Tacu tacu (rice-and-bean cake — leftover architecture)
Mayocoba (peruano/canary) beans soaked **8-24 h salted water**, simmered
**~3 h** until very soft and the liquid thickens to a **creamy glaze**. Flavor
base: bacon ~2 min, onion ~5 min, then **1 tbsp achiote paste + 1 tbsp ají
amarillo paste + ½ tsp dried oregano, 2 min**; stir into beans. Rice: toast garlic
in oil ~1 min (no color), add 1 cup rice, coat, 1¼ cups water, boil, cover, low
**15 min**, rest 10. Cake: **1 cup cooked beans with their creamy liquid +
1-1.5 cups cooked rice** (less rice if the bean liquid is thick); press into a
thick disc in a nonstick skillet over **medium-high**; **~6 min per side**,
pressing with a spatula; flip. Serve with zarza (salsa criolla) and/or fried egg,
seared steak, leftover stew.
- **Zarza/salsa criolla:** thinly sliced red onion + julienned ají amarillo +
  cilantro, tossed just before serving with lime juice, olive oil, salt, pepper,
  pinch cumin.
- **Fails:** cake falls apart (bean liquid too thin / too much rice — the glaze
  is the binder); burnt outside raw inside (heat too high); bland (no
  achiote/ají sofrito).

### 13. Escabeche (fried fish + vinegar-onion relish)
Season firm white fish (dorado preferred), dust with flour, fry in hot oil over
**medium**, golden, **~10-15 min** by size. Relish: ¼ cup oil; sliced onions
~1 min; then yellow chili strips (seeded), crushed garlic, 1 tsp yellow chili
powder, 1 tsp panca powder, salt, pepper, cumin; a few more minutes; then **¼ cup
red wine vinegar**, finish with oregano, heat OFF. Plate lettuce, fish, generous
onion sauce; corn, sweet potato, egg, olives; rice optional. **Served COLD —
better the day after.**
- **Fails:** bitter raw-flour crust (undercook); soggy fish (crowded pan); raw
  onion bite (relish needs its minutes); harsh vinegar (add it then let it marry
  off-heat).

### 14. Sancochado (the boil-everything hotpot)
1.5 kg beef brisket or ribs cut in **LARGE pieces (max 4-inch)** · 10-12 cups
water · 1 white onion · 2 large garlic cloves · chiles · mint + coriander ·
1 bouillon cube · 1 tbsp salt · potato, sweet potato, yucca, carrot, green beans,
cabbage, chickpeas. **~3 hours.** Serve TWO plates: a deep bowl of broth with
chopped cilantro, plus a flat plate with a big piece of each tuber/veg, meat, and
2 tbsp chickpeas. Rest the pot ~15 min before serving.
- **Fails:** cloudy starchy broth (**keep everything in big pieces** — small cuts
  release starch and thicken it); greasy broth (skim); raw cabbage; bland (the
  mint + coriander + bouillon are doing real work).

### 15. Ají de gallina (creamy ají chicken — never re-cook the chicken)
Poach 1 skinless breast in 3 cups salted water, uncovered, high, **~20 min**;
shred; strain stock. Soak 3 slices white bread in 1 cup stock; blend to a loose
paste. Sweat ½ finely chopped red onion + 2 garlic in ¼ cup oil over medium
**~10 min WITHOUT BROWNING** until very soft; add 1 tbsp ají amarillo + 1 tbsp
ají mirasol + ½ tsp oregano, cook 5 min; add bread paste + 1 cup stock, stir
~3 min until thick; add shredded chicken + ½ cup chopped pecans + ½ cup grated
Parmesan (watch salt); finish **off-heat** with ½ cup evaporated milk. Thin with
stock — the sauce should be velvety.
- **CRITICAL: after the chicken goes in, do not boil it** — heat briefly to
  piping hot only, or it turns stringy.
- **Fails:** stringy chicken (boiled after combining); broken sauce (boiling the
  evaporated milk); scorched garlic/onion (the 10-min sweat must not brown —
  browning tastes like onion soup); greasy separation (bread paste not fully
  blended).

### 16. Pisco sour (acid + egg-white foam benchmark)
**2 oz pisco · 2 oz simple syrup · 2 oz lime juice · 1 egg white · 2 drops
Angostura · ice.** Order: pisco → syrup → lime → egg white. Shake **10 seconds**
(or blender 5 s). Serve in two fills.
- **Fails:** no foam (under-shaken with ice); separating foam (over-diluted);
  cloying (the 2:2:2 is sweet — pull syrup toward 1.5 oz for drier palates).

## Signature dishes — how the techniques stack

1. **Ceviche clásico.** Leche de tigre (1) → ¼-inch slices or 2-cm cubes (2) →
   10-15 min cure, or 5-10 min per Peruvian orthodoxy → mandatory boiled choclo
   + sweet potato on the plate → salsa criolla / ají limo fresh heat at the table.
   An exercise in acid timing + ice-cold discipline + starch/sweet counterweight.
2. **Lomo saltado.** Chifa in miniature: native potato/tomato/ají + Chinese wok
   and soy + Spanish beef and rice. Against-the-grain steak (8) → smoke-hot wok,
   small batches, keep the juices → onion char-crisp, tomatoes last → soy +
   vinegar finish → fries + rice architecture.
3. **Ají de gallina.** Ají amarillo + mirasol pastes built on a no-color onion/
   garlic sweat (15) → bread-stock paste thickener → nuts + cheese + evaporated
   milk off-heat → never re-cook the chicken → potato + rice + egg/olive plate.

## Pitfalls (cross-cutting)

- Never over-squeeze limes and never juice them far ahead — pith makes it bitter.
- Never let lime sit on boiling-hot potato (causa) or let the cured fish sit past
  its window (ceviche).
- Never boil after adding evaporated milk (huancaína, ocopa, ají de gallina).
- Never crowd the pan in lomo saltado — batch it and keep the juices.
- Never use ají panca expecting heat, or huacatay expecting subtlety.
- Never re-cook chicken once it's in the ají de gallina sauce.
