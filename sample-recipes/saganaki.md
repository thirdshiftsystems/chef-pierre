# Saganaki (Greek Flaming Pan Cheese — Detroit Style)

**Serves:** 2–4 as an appetizer · **Time:** 10 min · **Difficulty:** medium (fire involved) · **Format:** cast-iron or heavy skillet
**Why it's here:** Detroit has one of the largest Greek communities in America, and saganaki — cheese seared and flamed tableside with a shout of "Opa!" — is the city's great appetizer. Crust outside, molten inside, lemon to finish.

## Ingredients

- 1 slab (about 8 oz, ¾–1 inch thick) of a searing cheese: **kefalograviera** or **kasseri** are traditional; **halloumi** or full-fat low-moisture mozzarella are good stand-ins
- ¼ cup all-purpose flour
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 2 tbsp brandy or ouzo (for flaming — optional but correct)
- 1 lemon, cut into wedges
- Crusty bread or pita, for serving

## Method

1. Pat the cheese completely dry, then dredge all sides in flour, shaking off excess. A thin, even coat is the crust — thick flour gets pasty.
2. Heat the olive oil in a heavy skillet over medium-high until it shimmers.
3. Sear the cheese undisturbed 2 minutes until deep golden, then flip and sear the second side 1–2 minutes more.
4. **The flame:** pull the pan off the heat, pour the brandy over the cheese, and ignite with a long match or lighter. ("Opa!" is mandatory.) Let the flame burn out on its own, 10–15 seconds.
   - *No-fire version:* skip the brandy entirely and go straight to lemon — still excellent, less theater.
5. Squeeze fresh lemon over the top and serve immediately, straight from the pan, with bread to catch the molten edges.

## Notes

- Eat it within minutes — saganaki waits for no one. The window from molten to rubbery is about 5 minutes.
- A sprinkle of toasted sesame seeds or a drizzle of honey after the flame is a sweet-savory variation worth knowing.
