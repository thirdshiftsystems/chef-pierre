# Detroit-Style Coney Dogs

**Serves:** 4 · **Time:** 45 min (mostly simmering) · **Difficulty:** easy · **Format:** stovetop pot + grill or skillet
**Why it's here:** Detroit's founding food. Two rival chains (American Coney Island and Lafayette, side by side downtown) have served the same blueprint since 1917: natural-casing dog in a soft bun, under a meaty no-bean chili that's closer to a sauce than a stew, then yellow mustard and a snowfall of diced white onion. The chili is the whole recipe — make it right.

## Ingredients

**Coney sauce (makes enough for 8 dogs — it's better as a double batch):**
- 1 lb ground beef (85/15) — or half beef / half ground lamb for the old-school flavor
- 1 cup finely diced white onion (plus more for serving)
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 1½ tbsp chili powder
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- 1 tsp paprika (or ½ tsp sweet + ½ tsp smoked)
- ½ tsp onion powder
- ½ tsp garlic powder
- 1½ tsp kosher salt, to taste
- ½ tsp black pepper
- Pinch of cayenne
- 2 pinches of sugar
- 1 can (15 oz) tomato **puree** (not sauce, not crushed — puree gives the right smooth-body)
- 3–4 tbsp prepared yellow mustard (yes, in the sauce — this is the secret)
- ¼ cup water

**Assembly:**
- 8 natural-casing hot dogs (the snap is non-negotiable)
- 8 soft hot dog buns
- Yellow mustard, for topping
- Finely diced white onion, for topping

## Method

1. **Sauce:** put the ground beef, diced onion, and garlic in a medium pot over medium heat. Break the meat into very fine crumbles — coney sauce is granular, not chunky. Cook until no pink remains.
2. Do **not** drain the fat. Add the spices, sugar, tomato puree, mustard, and water. Stir to combine.
3. Cover, reduce to low, and simmer 30 minutes, stirring occasionally. Uncover and simmer 10–15 more until it's thick enough to sit on a dog without running off. Taste for salt.
4. **Dogs:** grill or pan-sear the hot dogs until browned and snappy, 5–8 minutes, turning. Warm the buns on the grill or in a dry skillet, split side down.
5. **Assemble:** dog in bun, chili spooned generously over the top (it should bury the dog), a squiggle of yellow mustard, then a heavy sprinkle of diced white onion.
6. Serve immediately. Coney sauce also freezes well — make the double batch.

## Notes

- **Natural casing or nothing.** Skinless dogs make it a different, sadder food.
- The old Greeks downtown built the sauce on a beef-suet roux; the mustard-in-the-sauce trick here gets you most of the way there at home.
- Chili, mustard, and onion — in that order, always.
