# Detroit-Style Deep Dish Pizza

**Serves:** 4 (one 9×13 pan) · **Time:** ~3.5 hours (mostly rising) · **Difficulty:** medium · **Format:** oven, heavy metal pan
**Why it's here:** born in Detroit's auto plants — the original pans were blue steel parts trays from the factories — this is focaccia masquerading as pizza: airy, olive-oil-fried crust, cheese all the way to the edges (caramelized into a crisp lace), and sauce striped ON TOP of the cheese. Cut into squares. There is no round Detroit pizza.

## Ingredients

**Dough:**
- 3¾ cups (450 g) bread flour
- 1¾ cups (410 g) cool water
- 2 tsp instant yeast
- 2 tsp kosher salt
- 1 tsp sugar
- 2 tbsp olive oil, plus ~3 tbsp more for the pan (this is the fry-layer — be generous)

**Sauce:**
- 1 can (28 oz) crushed tomatoes
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 1 tbsp dried oregano
- 2 tsp dried basil
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- 1 tsp onion powder
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- 2 tsp sugar
- ¼ tsp black pepper

**Topping:**
- 12 oz whole-milk low-moisture mozzarella, shredded
- 6–8 oz Wisconsin brick cheese or mild provolone, shredded (the authentic choice; all-mozzarella works)
- 4–5 oz pepperoni or cup-and-char pepperoni (optional but encouraged)

## Method

1. **Dough:** stir flour, water, yeast, salt, and sugar into a shaggy mass. Knead by mixer (dough hook, medium-low ~10 min) or by hand until smooth, soft, and elastic. Cover and rise 1 hour, until puffy.
2. **Pan:** pour ~3 tbsp olive oil into a heavy 9×13 metal pan (dark steel is ideal — that's the fried-crust engine). Spread to coat the bottom AND sides.
3. Press the dough into the pan, oiling your hands if it fights back. It will spring back — cover, rest 10–15 min, and press again until it reaches the corners. Cover and rise 1 hour more, until pillowy and nearly doubled.
4. Meanwhile: **sauce** — simmer all sauce ingredients over medium-low for 15 minutes, stirring. It should be thick and spoonable, not runny. Heat the oven as hot as it goes (500–550°F) with a rack in the **bottom third**.
5. **Top in the Detroit order:** dimple the dough with oiled fingers. Pepperoni first (if using), then BOTH cheeses in an even layer — crucially, pushing cheese all the way to the pan walls so it melts into the crust edges. Spoon the sauce over the cheese in 3 lengthwise stripes (the classic look — the sauce stays on top).
6. **Bake** 12–15 min at 550°F (or 18–22 at 500°F) until the edges are deep brown and the exposed cheese is bubbling with golden-brown spots.
7. **Rest 10 minutes** — non-negotiable, or the cheese slides. Run a thin spatula around the walls, lift the pizza out onto a board (the oil-fried bottom should be crisp and golden), cut into squares.

## Notes

- **The caramelized cheese wall** is the signature — don't leave a gap between cheese and pan.
- A cast-iron skillet works if you don't have a steel pan (smaller pizza, same rules).
- Sauce-on-top is not a mistake. It steams the cheese below while keeping the crust crisp. Detroit doesn't do it backward.
- Leftovers reheat best in a dry skillet, crust-side down, covered.
